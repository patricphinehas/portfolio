Mixture and alligation

Alligation is a shortcut for mixing two rates to get a required average rate. It is used for prices, percentages of acid, and milk-and-water replacements. The rule is an inverse ratio: the cheaper item is used in a larger amount when the mean is closer to the cheaper price.

If you forget the cross, write the mixture equation. It is the same arithmetic and harder to mix up.

What SSC lists

  • Mixture and alligation of two prices or two strengths.
  • Finding the ratio of quantities for a required mean price.
  • Adding water, which has price or strength zero.
  • Changing the ratio in an existing mixture by adding one component.

Rules and formulas

Mean price

If quantities n1 and n2 have prices p1 and p2, mean = (n1×p1 + n2×p2) / (n1 + n2).

Alligation cross

Quantity of cheaper : quantity of dearer = (dearer − mean) : (mean − cheaper).
The mean must lie between the two prices.

Water

Treat pure water as price 0, or as 0% of the paid ingredient, when the question is about price or strength.

Replacement

If a vessel has V units and r units are removed and replaced, once, the quantity of the original liquid left is V × (1 − r/V).
After n such operations it is V × (1 − r/V)^n.

Quick methods

Repeated replacement: after n removals of r litres from V litres, the original liquid left = V × (1 − r/V)ⁿ.
Adding one component: the other component does not change, so it fixes the new total.
Mixing equal quantities of two mixtures: add the milk fractions a/(a+b) + c/(c+d), add the water fractions, then compare.
Alligation also works for group averages, speeds over equal times, and profit percents.

Common traps

The ratio from the cross is cheaper to dearer, in that order. Reversing it is the usual wrong option.

Water is the cheaper component when milk is being diluted by price.

Adding water does not change the amount of milk already in the vessel.

The mean cannot be outside the two given prices. If your mean is, the question has been misread.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
In what ratio should rice at Rs 30 per kg be mixed with rice at Rs 40 per kg so that the mixture is worth Rs 36 per kg?

  1. 2:3
  2. 3:2
  3. 1:2
  4. 3:4

Solution

  1. Cheaper is 30, dearer is 40, mean is 36.
  2. Cheaper : dearer = (40 − 36) : (36 − 30) = 4:6.
  3. Simplify to 2:3.

Answer: A. Two parts of the cheaper rice go with three parts of the dearer rice.

Example 2
Water is mixed with milk costing Rs 48 per litre so that the mixture is worth Rs 36 per litre. The ratio of water to milk is

  1. 1:3
  2. 3:1
  3. 1:4
  4. 3:4

Solution

  1. Water has price 0. Milk has price 48. Mean is 36.
  2. Water : milk = (48 − 36) : (36 − 0) = 12:36 = 1:3.

Answer: A. One litre of water with three litres of milk: cost 144 for 4 litres, which is 36 per litre.

Example 3
A 40-litre mixture of milk and water is in the ratio 3:1. How much water must be added to make the ratio 3:2?

  1. 5 litres
  2. 8 litres
  3. 10 litres
  4. 12 litres

Solution

  1. Milk = 3/4 of 40 = 30 litres. Water = 10 litres.
  2. Milk stays 30. For a 3:2 ratio, water must be 20 litres.
  3. Water to add = 10 litres.

Answer: C. The new mixture is 30 litres milk and 20 litres water.

Example 4
Solutions of 20% acid and 50% acid are mixed to get a 30% acid solution. The ratio of the 20% solution to the 50% solution is

  1. 1:2
  2. 2:1
  3. 3:2
  4. 1:1

Solution

  1. 20% is cheaper in acid, 50% is stronger, mean is 30%.
  2. Quantity of 20% : quantity of 50% = (50 − 30) : (30 − 20) = 20:10 = 2:1.

Answer: B. Two litres of the weaker solution with one litre of the stronger one: acid = 0.4 + 0.5 = 0.9 in 3 litres, which is 30%.

Example 5
20 kg of rice at Rs 18 per kg is mixed with 30 kg at Rs 28 per kg. The price per kg of the mixture is

  1. Rs 22
  2. Rs 24
  3. Rs 25
  4. Rs 26

Solution

  1. Cost = 20 × 18 + 30 × 28 = 360 + 840 = 1200.
  2. Total weight = 50 kg.
  3. Mean price = 1200/50 = 24.

Answer: B. The larger heap is the dearer one, so the mean sits closer to 28 than a simple midpoint would.

Example 6
(CGL pattern) One man adds 5 litres of water to 15 litres of milk. Another adds 3 litres of water to 7 litres of milk. The ratio of the proportion of milk in the two mixtures is

  1. 15:14
  2. 14:15
  3. 5:7
  4. 3:7

Solution

  1. First mixture: milk = 15/20 = 3/4.
  2. Second mixture: milk = 7/10.
  3. 3/4 : 7/10 = 30:28 = 15:14.

Answer: A. Compare fractions of milk, not the litres themselves.

  2. 2:1

  4. 3:2

Answer: A. (35−25):(25−15) = 10:10 = 1:1.

  1. 4 litres
  2. 12 litres
  3. 16 litres
  4. 18 litres
Show answer

Answer: C. Milk is 4/5 of 20 = 16 litres.

Practice 4
10 litres of water are added to 30 litres of pure milk. The ratio of milk to water becomes

  1. 3:1
  2. 1:3
  3. 4:1
  4. 2:1
Show answer

Answer: A. Milk stays 30 and water is 10, so the ratio is 3:1.

Practice 5
A vessel has 40 L of milk. 4 L is removed and replaced with water, twice. The milk left is

  1. 32 L
  2. 32.4 L
  3. 36 L
  4. 36.4 L
Show answer

Answer: B. 40 × (1 − 4/40)² = 40 × 0.81 = 32.4.

Practice 6
Boys average 50 kg and girls 40 kg. The class average is 46 kg. Boys : girls is

  1. 3:2
  2. 2:3
  3. 1:1
  4. 4:3
Show answer

Answer: A. (46 − 40) : (50 − 46) = 6:4 = 3:2.

Practice 7
Equal quantities of two mixtures, milk:water 3:1 and 1:1, are combined. The new ratio is

  1. 2:1
  2. 3:5
  3. 5:3
  4. 4:1
Show answer

Answer: C. Milk = 3/4 + 1/2 = 5/4. Water = 1/4 + 1/2 = 3/4. Ratio 5:3.

Practice 8
Sugar at Rs 20 and Rs 30 per kg is mixed to sell at a mean of Rs 24. The ratio cheaper : dearer is

  1. 2:3
  2. 3:2
  3. 3:4
  4. 1:1
Show answer

Answer: B. (30 − 24) : (24 − 20) = 6:4 = 3:2.

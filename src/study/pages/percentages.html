Quantitative Aptitude · Tier-I and Paper-I

Percentages

Percent means per hundred. In the paper it is usually a fraction with denominator 100, hidden inside marks, price, population or a comparison of two people.

Switch to a fraction before you multiply. If you know that 12.5% is 1/8, the arithmetic gets shorter.

What SSC lists

  • Finding a percentage of a quantity, and a quantity as a percentage of another.
  • Increase, decrease and successive percentage changes.
  • Comparison: if A is r% more than B, by what percent is B less than A.
  • Population growth, price and consumption, and simple election percentages.

Rules and formulas

Core

x% of y = (x/100) × y.
What percent of x is y? (y/x) × 100.

One change

Increase by r%: multiply by (100 + r)/100.
Decrease by r%: multiply by (100 − r)/100.

Reverse comparison

If A is r% more than B, B is less than A by [r / (100 + r)] × 100 %.
If A is r% less than B, B is more than A by [r / (100 − r)] × 100 %.

Two changes

Successive changes of a% and b%, taking a fall as negative, give net percent a + b + (a×b)/100.
If price rises by r% and expenditure stays the same, consumption becomes 100/(100+r) of the old consumption.

Quick methods

Fraction table: 1/2 = 50%, 1/3 = 33⅓%, 1/4 = 25%, 1/5 = 20%, 1/6 = 16⅔%, 1/7 ≈ 14.28%, 1/8 = 12.5%, 1/9 ≈ 11.11%, 1/10 = 10%, 1/11 ≈ 9.09%, 1/12 = 8⅓%.
Population after n years at r% growth = P × (1 + r/100)ⁿ, the same shape as compound interest.
If price falls by r%, consumption can rise by [r/(100 − r)] × 100 % for the same spending.
Pass-mark questions: (marks obtained + marks short) = pass percent of the maximum.

Common traps

A rise of 20% does not mean the reverse comparison is also 20%. The base has changed.

A 20% rise and then a 20% fall leaves you 4% down, not back at the start.

Read the base: “what percent of A is B” divides by A.

In this syllabus, profit percent is on cost price unless the question clearly says otherwise.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
If A is 25% more than B, then B is less than A by

  1. 20%
  2. 25%
  3. 16⅔%
  4. 30%

Solution

  1. Take B as 100. Then A is 125.
  2. The gap is 25, and the base is now A.
  3. (25/125) × 100 = 20%.

Answer: A. The reverse-comparison formula gives [25/125] × 100 = 20%.

Example 2
A number is increased by 20% and then decreased by 20%. The net change is

  1. no change
  2. 4% increase
  3. 4% decrease
  4. 2% decrease

Solution

  1. Net percent = 20 + (−20) + (20 × −20)/100.
  2. That is −4.
  3. The number falls by 4%.

Answer: C. Start at 100: it becomes 120, then 96.

Example 3
35% of a number is 84. The number is

  1. 200
  2. 220
  3. 240
  4. 280

Solution

  1. 35% is 7/20.
  2. (7/20) of the number = 84.
  3. Number = 84 × 20/7 = 240.

Answer: C. 35% of 240 is 84.

Example 4
In a two-candidate election one candidate got 55% of the votes and won by 800 votes. The total number of votes is

  1. 4000
  2. 8000
  3. 10000
  4. 16000

Solution

  1. The other candidate got 45%, so the margin is 10% of the total.
  2. 10% of the total = 800.
  3. Total = 800 × 100/10 = 8000.

Answer: B. The winner’s votes are 55% of 8000 = 4400, and 4400 − 3600 = 800.

Example 5
The price of sugar rises by 25%. By what percent must a family cut consumption so that expenditure does not change?

  1. 15%
  2. 20%
  3. 25%
  4. 30%

Solution

  1. New price is 5/4 of the old price.
  2. Consumption must become 4/5 of the old amount.
  3. The cut is 1/5, which is 20%.

Answer: B. On a base of 100 units of consumption, the new consumption is 80.

Example 6
(CGL pattern) In an election, 80% of enrolled voters voted and 5% of the votes cast were invalid. The winner got 45,600 votes, which was 60% of the valid votes. The number of enrolled voters is

  1. 1,00,000
  2. 95,000
  3. 1,20,000
  4. 80,000

Solution

  1. Valid votes = 45600 / 0.6 = 76000.
  2. Votes cast = 76000 / 0.95 = 80000.
  3. Enrolled = 80000 / 0.8 = 100000.

Answer: A. Work backwards through each percentage, one base at a time.

Practice yourself

Practice 1
What percent of 80 is 20?

  1. 15%
  2. 20%
  3. 25%
  4. 40%
Show answer

Answer: C. (20/80) × 100 = 25%.

Practice 2
A price falls by 10% and then rises by 10%. The net change is

  1. 0%
  2. 1% fall
  3. 1% rise
  4. 10% fall
Show answer

Answer: B. 10 + (−10) + (10)(−10)/100 = −1.

Practice 3
If 40% of a number is 48 more than 25% of it, the number is

  1. 280
  2. 300
  3. 320
  4. 360
Show answer

Answer: C. The gap is 15% of the number, so the number is 48 × 100/15 = 320.

Practice 4
A’s income is 20% less than B’s. By what percent is B’s income more than A’s?

  1. 16%
  2. 20%
  3. 25%
  4. 30%
Show answer

Answer: C. If B is 100, A is 80. The gap of 20 is 25% of 80.

Practice 5
12.5% of 640 is

  1. 64
  2. 72
  3. 80
  4. 96
Show answer

Answer: C. 12.5% = 1/8, and 640/8 = 80.

Practice 6
A town of 10000 grows by 10% a year. After 2 years its population is

  1. 12000
  2. 12100
  3. 12200
  4. 12210
Show answer

Answer: B. 10000 × 1.1 × 1.1 = 12100.

Practice 7
The price of rice falls by 20%. By what percent can a family increase consumption without spending more?

  1. 20%
  2. 25%
  3. 16⅔%
  4. 30%
Show answer

Answer: B. 20/(100 − 20) × 100 = 25%.

Practice 8
A student needs 40% to pass. He scores 160 and fails by 40 marks. The maximum marks are

  1. 400
  2. 450
  3. 500
  4. 600
Show answer

Answer: C. Pass mark = 200 = 40% of the maximum, so the maximum is 500.

Practice 9
Successive increases of 10% and 20% equal a single increase of

  1. 30%
  2. 31%
  3. 32%
  4. 33%
Show answer

Answer: C. 10 + 20 + (10 × 20)/100 = 32%.

English · Tier-I and Tier-II English

Fill in the blanks

A blank is a missing word that grammar or meaning demands. Look at the word just before the blank and the word just after it. An article depends on the sound that follows. A preposition of time depends on whether the word after it is a point or a length.

If two options are both real words, the grammar of the sentence still has to accept only one.

What SSC lists

  • Fill in the blanks, in a sentence or a short passage.
  • Articles, prepositions, tense helpers and agreement.
  • A vocabulary blank, where the meaning of the sentence selects the word.
  • The same skill as the cloze test, on one blank at a time.

Rules and formulas

Articles

Use a before a consonant sound and an before a vowel sound.
An hour, an honest man, because the h is silent. A university, because the first sound is “you”.

Time

Since goes with a point: since 2020, since Monday.
For goes with a length: for three years.

Agreement in the blank

Neither of the two takes a singular verb.
If I were you uses were for a present hypothetical.

Meaning

When the options are synonyms, the sentence’s tone still picks one. A formal report does not “grab” a fact if “states” is available and the tone is formal.

Common traps

Choose the article by sound, not by spelling. Honest starts with a vowel sound.

Since is not used with a length of time.

Were in “if I were you” is not a past story. It is a hypothetical.

Do not fill a blank with a word that duplicates a word already in the sentence.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
He is ______ honest man.

  1. a
  2. an
  3. the
  4. no article

Solution

  1. Honest begins with a silent h, so the first sound is a vowel.
  2. The article is an.

Answer: B. A honest would match the spelling and miss the sound.

Example 2
She has been working here ______ 2020.

  1. for
  2. since
  3. from
  4. by

Solution

  1. 2020 is a point in time, and the verb is in the present perfect.
  2. The preposition is since.

Answer: B. For would need a length, such as for six years.

Example 3
Neither of the two plans ______ acceptable.

  1. are
  2. were
  3. is
  4. have

Solution

  1. Neither of the two is a singular subject.
  2. The verb is is.

Answer: C. The word two does not make the verb plural.

Example 4
If I ______ you, I would apologise.

  1. was
  2. were
  3. am
  4. have been

Solution

  1. The sentence imagines something untrue in the present.
  2. The form used here is were.

Answer: B. Would in the result clause matches this hypothetical pattern.

Example 5
The committee ______ divided on the proposal. Take the committee as one body.

  1. are
  2. is
  3. were being
  4. have

Solution

  1. Treated as one body, a committee takes a singular verb.
  2. The verb is is.

Answer: B. If the question instead says the members disagree among themselves, a plural verb can be used. Here the instruction says one body.

Example 6
(CGL pattern) You can ______ call me or send a message.

  1. neither
  2. either
  3. not only
  4. had better

Solution

  1. The sentence has or, so it needs either.
  2. Neither pairs with nor.

Answer: B. Not only pairs with but also.

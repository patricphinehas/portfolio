Biology for General Awareness

Biology gives more General Awareness marks than the other sciences for most candidates, because vitamins, diseases and the human body are easy to set as one-line questions. Learn them as pairs: vitamin and deficiency, organ and job, germ and the kind of germ.

A current outbreak belongs in your weekly affairs notes. The pairs on this page are the stable ones.

What SSC lists

  • Human body, health and other school biology inside General Awareness.
  • Vitamins, blood, heart and hormones.
  • A few diseases and the type of organism that causes them.
  • Photosynthesis and the cell, at the level of a definition.

Rules and formulas

Vitamins

Vitamin A: night blindness. B1: beriberi. C: scurvy. D: rickets. K: trouble with clotting. B12: pernicious anaemia in the standard pairing.
These are deficiency pairings used in the exam, not a medical diagnosis chart.

Body

The human heart has four chambers.
Mature red blood cells of mammals have no nucleus.
Insulin is produced by the pancreas. Thyroxine is a thyroid hormone.
In the school answer, group O is the universal donor for red-cell transfusion and group AB is the universal recipient. Whole-blood transfusion is more restricted in real hospitals. The exam still uses the school line.

Germs

Tuberculosis: bacterium (Mycobacterium). Cholera: bacterium (Vibrio cholerae). Malaria: Plasmodium, a parasite, spread by the Anopheles mosquito. AIDS: HIV, a virus. Dengue: a virus, spread by Aedes mosquitoes.

Plants and cells

Photosynthesis, in green plants, uses carbon dioxide and water in the presence of light and chlorophyll and produces glucose, with oxygen released.
Plant cells have a cell wall. Animal cells do not.

Common traps

Malaria is not a virus and not a bacterium. It is a parasitic infection.

Vitamin C is scurvy, vitamin D is rickets. Swapping them is the usual error.

Insulin is the pancreas, not the thyroid.

The universal-donor line is the school answer for red cells. Do not extend it to every blood product if a question becomes more precise.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
Deficiency of vitamin C causes

  1. rickets
  2. scurvy
  3. beriberi
  4. night blindness

Solution

  1. Vitamin C is paired with scurvy.
  2. Rickets is D, beriberi is B1, and night blindness is A.

Answer: B. Citrus fruit is the memory hook for C, not the scientific definition.

Example 2
Malaria is caused by

  1. a virus
  2. a bacterium
  3. Plasmodium
  4. a deficiency of vitamin K

Solution

  1. Plasmodium is a parasite.
  2. The Anopheles mosquito transmits it. The mosquito is the vector, not the cause written on the slide.

Answer: C. Dengue, by contrast, is a virus.

Example 3
Insulin is produced by the

  1. thyroid
  2. pancreas
  3. liver as its only hormone source
  4. kidney

Solution

  1. Insulin is a pancreatic hormone.
  2. Thyroxine belongs to the thyroid.

Answer: B. The liver stores glycogen and does many other jobs. It is not the source of insulin.

Example 4
Mature red blood cells in humans

  1. have several nuclei
  2. have no nucleus
  3. produce insulin
  4. carry chlorophyll

Solution

  1. Mammalian mature red cells lack a nucleus.
  2. That leaves more room for haemoglobin.

Answer: B. Chlorophyll belongs to green plants, not to blood.

Example 5
(CGL pattern) Which is NOT a characteristic of Aves (birds)?

  1. Four-chambered heart
  2. They give birth to live young
  3. Warm-blooded
  4. Breathe through lungs

Solution

  1. Birds lay eggs.
  2. The other three are true of birds.

Answer: B. Live birth is a mammal feature.

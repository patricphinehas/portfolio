General Awareness · Tier-I and Paper-I General Awareness

Indian polity

Polity in this exam is the Constitution as a working map: who holds which power, which article protects which right, and how a bill becomes law. You do not need a law degree. You need the articles and the institutions that papers ask again and again.

Names of current office-holders change. Articles do not, unless they are amended. Learn the articles here, and check any new amendment from a current source before the exam.

What SSC lists

  • India and its political system, under General Awareness.
  • The same polity is asked in Tier-I and in Paper-I.
  • Constitution, Parliament, executive, judiciary and local government.
  • A current-affairs layer sits on top: new bills and appointments need a fresh source.

Rules and formulas

Frame

The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 and came into force on 26 January 1950.
The Preamble describes India as a sovereign, socialist, secular, democratic republic. The words socialist and secular were added by the 42nd Amendment.
Fundamental Rights are in Articles 12 to 35. Directive Principles are in Articles 36 to 51. Fundamental Duties are in Article 51A.

Rights often asked

Article 14: equality before the law. Articles 14 to 18 cover equality more broadly.
Article 19: the freedoms of speech and expression, assembly, association, movement, residence, and profession.
Article 21: protection of life and personal liberty. Article 21A: free and compulsory education for ages 6 to 14, added by the 86th Amendment.
Article 32: right to move the Supreme Court for enforcement of Fundamental Rights.
Right to property is no longer a Fundamental Right. It is a legal right under Article 300A, after the 44th Amendment.

Institutions

The President is elected for five years by an electoral college and can be impeached under Article 61. The Prime Minister is appointed under Article 75 and is the real head of the Council of Ministers.
Lok Sabha’s normal term is five years. Rajya Sabha members sit for six years, with about one-third retiring every two years.
A Money Bill is defined in Article 110 and can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha.
The Election Commission is under Article 324. The Comptroller and Auditor General is under Article 148. The Finance Commission is under Article 280.

Emergencies and local government

Article 352: National Emergency. Article 356: failure of constitutional machinery in a state, often called President’s Rule. Article 360: Financial Emergency.
The 73rd Amendment deals with panchayats. The 74th deals with municipalities.
The Seventh Schedule has the Union, State and Concurrent Lists. The Tenth Schedule deals with disqualification on defection.

Common traps

Article 32 is the Supreme Court route. Article 226 is the High Court route for writs. Do not swap them.

The President appoints the Chief Justice of India under the constitutional text. In practice a collegium recommends. The exam’s short answer is still the President, unless the question is about the collegium.

Socialist and secular are in the Preamble now. They were not in the 1950 text.

Fundamental Duties were added later, by the 42nd Amendment. There are 11 duties in Article 51A. If a new amendment adds language to the Eighth Schedule, update that count from the Gazette. The figure long used in books is 22 languages.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
The Constitution of India came into force on

  1. 15 August 1947
  2. 26 November 1949
  3. 26 January 1950
  4. 26 January 1930

Solution

  1. 15 August 1947 is Independence.
  2. 26 November 1949 is the date of adoption.
  3. 26 January 1950 is the date it came into force.

Answer: C. Republic Day marks the commencement, not the adoption.

Example 2
Which article is called the right to constitutional remedies?

  1. Article 14
  2. Article 19
  3. Article 21
  4. Article 32

Solution

  1. Article 14 is equality before the law.
  2. Article 19 is freedoms, and Article 21 is life and personal liberty.
  3. Article 32 lets a person move the Supreme Court to enforce Fundamental Rights.

Answer: D. Dr B.R. Ambedkar described Article 32 as the heart and soul of the Constitution. That is a standard classroom line, not a sentence from the article itself.

Example 3
A Money Bill can be introduced in

  1. either House
  2. the Rajya Sabha only
  3. the Lok Sabha only
  4. a joint sitting only

Solution

  1. Article 110 defines a Money Bill.
  2. The Constitution allows its introduction only in the Lok Sabha.

Answer: C. The Rajya Sabha can recommend changes, within a limited time, but it is not the House of introduction.

Example 4
Panchayats were given constitutional status by the

  1. 42nd Amendment
  2. 44th Amendment
  3. 73rd Amendment
  4. 86th Amendment

Solution

  1. The 42nd Amendment is associated with the Emergency-era changes, including Fundamental Duties.
  2. The 73rd Amendment is the panchayat amendment.
  3. The 74th is municipalities, and the 86th includes Article 21A.

Answer: C. Keep 73rd and 74th as a pair: rural, then urban.

Example 5
(CGL pattern) The legislative organ of the Union government is

  1. Judiciary
  2. Legislative Assembly
  3. Executive
  4. Parliament

Solution

  1. Parliament makes Union laws.
  2. A Legislative Assembly is a state body.

Answer: D. Parliament = President + Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha.

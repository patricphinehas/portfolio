Microeconomics

Part B of Paper-III is Economics and Governance, for 120 marks. The microeconomics slice is demand, supply, production and the shapes of markets. The graphs are the ones from an undergraduate first course: a downward demand curve, an upward supply curve, and a production possibility curve.

Elasticity says how strongly quantity responds. The law of demand says in which direction it responds.

What SSC lists

  • Scope of economics, central problems and the production possibility curve.
  • Demand, supply and elasticity.
  • Marshallian utility and indifference curves, at the level of the idea.
  • Production, cost, and price in perfect competition, monopoly, monopolistic competition and oligopoly.

Rules and formulas

Basics

Scarcity forces a choice. The central problems are what to produce, how to produce, and for whom.
A production possibility curve shows the maximum combinations of two goods. A point inside is inefficient. A point outside is unattainable with present resources.
The curve bows outward when opportunity cost rises as you produce more of one good.

Demand and supply

The law of demand: price up, quantity demanded down, other things equal.
Price elasticity of demand = percentage change in quantity / percentage change in price. A result larger than 1, in absolute value, is elastic.
The law of supply: price up, quantity supplied up, other things equal.
Cross elasticity is the response of one good to the price of another. Income elasticity is the response to income.

The consumer

The Marshallian approach uses marginal utility and the idea that the consumer balances utility per rupee.
An indifference curve joins bundles that give the same satisfaction. A higher curve is better. The budget line is the limit set by income and prices.

Markets

Perfect competition: many sellers, a homogeneous product, free entry, and each firm is a price taker.
Monopoly: one seller, a price maker, with barriers to entry.
Monopolistic competition: many sellers and a differentiated product.
Oligopoly: a few sellers, each watching the others.
The law of variable proportions: as one input is increased and others are held fixed, marginal product eventually falls.

Common traps

Elasticity is not the slope in raw units. It is a ratio of percentage changes.

A price taker does not set the market price. A monopolist has that power, limited by the demand curve.

A point outside the production possibility curve is not “efficient”. It is unattainable.

Demand slopes down for the ordinary case. A higher price does not raise quantity demanded when the law’s assumptions hold.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
Other things equal, the law of demand says that a higher price leads to

  1. a higher quantity demanded
  2. a lower quantity demanded
  3. no change ever in the long run only
  4. a higher supply curve

Solution

  1. Price and quantity demanded move in opposite directions.
  2. The demand curve slopes downward.

Answer: B. Supply is the curve that slopes upward.

Example 2
If price rises by 10% and quantity demanded falls by 20%, price elasticity of demand is

  1. 0.5
  2. 2
  3. 10
  4. 30

Solution

  1. Elasticity = 20 / 10 = 2, in absolute value.
  2. A value above 1 is elastic.

Answer: B. 0.5 would be the result of dividing the wrong way.

Example 3
In perfect competition, a firm is a

  1. price maker
  2. price taker
  3. single seller
  4. regulator

Solution

  1. Many sellers and a homogeneous product.
  2. The firm takes the market price.

Answer: B. The single seller is the monopolist.

Example 4
A point inside the production possibility curve shows

  1. the maximum possible output
  2. unused or misused resources
  3. a monopoly price
  4. an index number

Solution

  1. The curve itself is the frontier.
  2. Inside it, more of one good could be made without giving up the other, or both could rise.

Answer: B. On the curve, producing more of one good costs some of the other.

Practice 1
An oligopoly has

  1. one seller
  2. a few sellers who watch each other
  3. innumerable sellers of an identical good and no strategy
  4. no prices
Show answer

Answer: B. A few interdependent sellers.

Practice 2
Monopolistic competition is marked by

  1. a single seller
  2. product differentiation and many sellers
  3. no advertising ever
  4. a vertical demand curve only
Show answer

Answer: B. Many sellers, but the product is not identical.

Practice 3
The law of variable proportions holds other inputs

  1. all increasing
  2. fixed, while one input varies
  3. at zero
  4. equal to price
Show answer

Answer: B. One variable input, the rest fixed. Marginal product eventually diminishes.

Practice 4
Income elasticity measures the response of demand to

  1. the price of the same good
  2. income
  3. a tax rate only
  4. the number of firms
Show answer

Answer: B. Percentage change in quantity over percentage change in income.

Practice 5
Price falls by 5% and quantity demanded rises by 10%. Price elasticity of demand is

  1. 0.5
  2. 2
  3. 5
  4. 15
Show answer

Answer: B. 10/5 = 2, elastic.

Practice 6
Income rises by 10% and demand rises by 5%. Income elasticity is

  1. 0.5
  2. 2
  3. −0.5
  4. 15
Show answer

Answer: A. 5/10 = 0.5, a normal necessity.

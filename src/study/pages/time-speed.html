Time, speed and distance

Distance, speed and time are one relation: distance = speed × time. The rest of the topic is unit conversion, two moving objects, trains whose length counts as distance, and boats in a stream.

Convert km/h to m/s before you mix metres with hours. Multiply by 5/18.

What SSC lists

  • Time and distance, including average speed.
  • Relative speed of two bodies moving in the same or opposite directions.
  • Trains crossing a pole, a person, or a platform.
  • Boats and streams: downstream and upstream.

Rules and formulas

Core

Distance = speed × time.
Speed = distance / time. Time = distance / speed.
km/h to m/s: multiply by 5/18. m/s to km/h: multiply by 18/5.

Average speed

For the same distance both ways, average speed = 2×s1×s2 / (s1 + s2).
This is not (s1 + s2)/2. Use total distance divided by total time when the distances differ.

Relative speed

Opposite directions: add the speeds.
Same direction: subtract the smaller from the larger.
A train crossing a pole covers its own length. Crossing a platform covers train length plus platform length.

Boats

Downstream speed = boat + stream. Upstream speed = boat − stream.
Boat in still water = (downstream + upstream) / 2.
Speed of the stream = (downstream − upstream) / 2.

Quick methods

Two trains crossing each other: time = (L₁ + L₂) / relative speed.
Same distance: if speed changes in the ratio a:b, time changes in the ratio b:a.
Late t₁ at speed s₁, early t₂ at speed s₂: distance = s₁s₂(t₁ + t₂)/(s₂ − s₁).
Two people D apart walking toward each other meet after D/(s₁ + s₂).
Train passing a man running the same way: use (train − man) speed and the train’s length.

Common traps

Average speed for two equal distances is the harmonic form, not the ordinary mean.

A pole has no length. A platform does.

54 km/h is 15 m/s, not 54 m/s.

If the stream is faster than the boat, upstream speed is not negative in a normal SSC question. Check the reading.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
A car runs at 60 km/h for 2 hours 30 minutes. The distance covered is

  1. 120 km
  2. 130 km
  3. 150 km
  4. 180 km

Solution

  1. 2 hours 30 minutes = 2.5 hours.
  2. Distance = 60 × 2.5 = 150 km.

Answer: C. Thirty minutes is half an hour, so it adds 30 km.

Example 2
A train 150 m long runs at 54 km/h. The time it takes to pass a pole is

  1. 8 s
  2. 10 s
  3. 12 s
  4. 15 s

Solution

  1. 54 km/h = 54 × 5/18 = 15 m/s.
  2. The train must cover its own length, 150 m.
  3. Time = 150/15 = 10 seconds.

Answer: B. A pole is a point, so the platform length is not added.

Example 3
The same 150 m train at 15 m/s crosses a 210 m platform in

  1. 16 s
  2. 20 s
  3. 24 s
  4. 30 s

Solution

  1. Distance to cover = 150 + 210 = 360 m.
  2. Time = 360/15 = 24 seconds.

Answer: C. Both lengths are part of the distance.

Example 4
A person goes to a town at 40 km/h and returns at 60 km/h. The average speed for the whole journey is

  1. 48 km/h
  2. 50 km/h
  3. 52 km/h
  4. 55 km/h

Solution

  1. The two distances are equal.
  2. Average speed = 2 × 40 × 60 / (40 + 60).
  3. 4800/100 = 48 km/h.

Answer: A. The ordinary mean of 40 and 60 is 50, and it is the trap option.

Example 5
A boat’s speed in still water is 12 km/h and the stream is 3 km/h. The time to go 45 km downstream is

  1. 2 hours
  2. 3 hours
  3. 4 hours
  4. 5 hours

Solution

  1. Downstream speed = 12 + 3 = 15 km/h.
  2. Time = 45/15 = 3 hours.

Answer: B. The same 45 km upstream would take 45/9 = 5 hours.

Example 6
(CGL pattern) A boat’s speed in still water is 15 km/h and the stream is 3 km/h. The time to go 72 km downstream and come back is

  1. 8 hours
  2. 9 hours
  3. 10 hours
  4. 12 hours

Solution

  1. Downstream 18 km/h: 72/18 = 4 h.
  2. Upstream 12 km/h: 72/12 = 6 h.
  3. Total = 10 h.

Answer: C. Do not use the still-water speed for the whole trip.

Practice yourself

Attempt these without opening the answer. Then check the toggle.

Practice 1
Convert 36 km/h into m/s.

  1. 8 m/s
  2. 10 m/s
  3. 12 m/s
  4. 15 m/s
Show answer

Answer: B. 36 × 5/18 = 10 m/s.

Practice 2
Two trains approach each other at 40 km/h and 50 km/h. Their relative speed is

  1. 10 km/h
  2. 45 km/h
  3. 90 km/h
  4. 2000 km/h
Show answer

Answer: C. Opposite directions: add the speeds, 90 km/h.

Practice 3
Walking at 5 km/h, a person covers 15 km in

  1. 2 h
  2. 2.5 h
  3. 3 h
  4. 4 h
Show answer

Answer: C. Time = 15/5 = 3 hours.

Practice 4
Downstream speed is 15 km/h and upstream speed is 9 km/h. The speed of the stream is

  1. 3 km/h
  2. 6 km/h
  3. 12 km/h
  4. 24 km/h
Show answer

Answer: A. Stream = (15 − 9) / 2 = 3 km/h. The boat in still water is 12 km/h.

Practice 5
Trains 120 m and 180 m long run in opposite directions at 40 and 50 km/h. They cross each other in

  1. 10 s
  2. 12 s
  3. 15 s
  4. 18 s
Show answer

Answer: B. Relative speed 90 km/h = 25 m/s. 300/25 = 12 s.

Practice 6
Two towns are 300 km apart. Cars leave each town toward the other at 40 and 60 km/h. They meet after

  1. 3 hours
  2. 2 hours
  3. 5 hours
  4. 7.5 hours
Show answer

Answer: A. 300/(40 + 60) = 3 hours.

Practice 7
Two speeds are in the ratio 3:4 over the same distance. The faster takes 30 minutes. The slower takes

  1. 35 min
  2. 40 min
  3. 45 min
  4. 22.5 min
Show answer

Answer: B. Times are in the ratio 4:3, so 30 × 4/3 = 40 minutes.

Practice 8
At 4 km/h a man is 10 minutes late; at 5 km/h he is 5 minutes early. The distance is

  1. 4 km
  2. 6 km
  3. 5 km
  4. 7.5 km
Show answer

Answer: C. 4 × 5 × (15/60) / (5 − 4) = 5 km. Check: 75 min vs 60 min.

Practice 9
A train at 42 km/h passes a man running at 6 km/h in the same direction in 10 seconds. The train is

  1. 100 m
  2. 120 m
  3. 150 m
  4. 200 m
Show answer

Answer: A. Relative speed 36 km/h = 10 m/s. 10 × 10 = 100 m.

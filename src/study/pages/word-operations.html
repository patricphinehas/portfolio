Reasoning · Tier-I and Paper-I

Word building, signs and matching

The rest of the official reasoning list is a set of short clerical and judgment items: make a word from letters, replace a sign by another operation, match an address or a date, and pick a sensible response. None of these needs a long method. Each needs one careful reading.

Emotional intelligence and social intelligence, which the notice names, are situation questions. The useful answer is the one that notices the other person’s state before it jumps to advice.

What SSC lists

  • Word building from a jumble of letters.
  • Numerical and symbolic operations, where a sign is redefined.
  • Indexing, address matching, date and city matching, and centre-code checks.
  • Drawing inferences, critical thinking, and a plain reading of emotional or social situations.

Rules and formulas

Word building

Try a vowel as the centre of the word. Rearrange the consonants around it.
A jumble is solved when it spells one dictionary word, not a second private abbreviation.

Redefined signs

Replace every sign before you calculate.
If + means ×, then do the multiplication where you see +.
Do the new operations in the order the question states. If it is silent, use ordinary order after the replacement.

Matching

Compare two strings character by character: house number, city spelling, PIN, date.
The answer is the line where they differ, or the line where they match, exactly as asked.

Judgment

An inference must be supported by the sentence, not by a likely extra story.
In a situation item, the better response usually acknowledges the person and then acts. Insulting, ignoring, or preaching is the weak option.

Common traps

A redefined plus sign is not addition any more. Do not “partly” use the old meaning.

In address matching, “Road” and “Rd” may be printed as a mismatch if every character must match. Read how strict the question is.

An inference that could be true is not an inference that must be true.

A situation question is still one mark. Do not hunt for a trick if one option is clearly respectful and useful.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
Which jumble makes a meaningful English word?

  1. EACR
  2. QZXJ
  3. VKQP
  4. XJZQ

Solution

  1. EACR rearranges to CARE or RACE.
  2. The other three strings do not rearrange into an English word.

Answer: A. Both CARE and RACE use exactly the letters E, A, C and R.

Example 2
If + means ×, − means +, and × means −, then 6 + 2 − 3 equals

  1. 9
  2. 15
  3. 18
  4. 5

Solution

  1. Replace the signs first: 6 × 2 + 3.
  2. 6 × 2 = 12, and 12 + 3 = 15.

Answer: B. The written minus becomes a plus. It is not subtracted.

Example 3
Two address lines are compared. Line 1: 14, MG Road, Pune 411001. Line 2: 14, MG Road, Pune 411002. They differ in the

  1. house number
  2. street
  3. city
  4. PIN code

Solution

  1. House number 14 matches.
  2. MG Road matches, and Pune matches.
  3. The PIN codes 411001 and 411002 differ.

Answer: D. Compare one field at a time, from the left.

Example 4
A passage says: “Every student in the room handed in a notebook. Meera was in the room.” Which inference follows?

  1. Meera handed in a notebook
  2. Meera is the class monitor
  3. The notebooks were checked the same day
  4. Meera arrived late

Solution

  1. The first sentence covers every student in the room.
  2. Meera was in the room, so she is included.
  3. The other options add facts the passage does not give.

Answer: A. This is a must-be-true inference, not a guess about Meera’s role.

Example 5
(CGL pattern) If + and − are interchanged and × and ÷ are interchanged, 20 ÷ 3 − 48 × 8 + 14 equals

  1. 46
  2. 52
  3. 58
  4. 40

Solution

  1. Rewrite: 20 × 3 + 48 ÷ 8 − 14.
  2. 60 + 6 − 14 = 52.

Answer: B. Swap every sign first, then apply BODMAS.

Practice yourself

Attempt these without opening the answer. Then check the toggle.

Practice 1
If × means +, and + means ×, then 4 + 5 × 2 equals

  1. 14
  2. 22
  3. 30
  4. 40
Show answer

Answer: B. After replacement the expression is 4 × 5 + 2 = 22.

Practice 2
The letters NTRA rearrange to a meaningful word. The word is

  1. RANT
  2. TRNA
  3. NART
  4. ARTN
Show answer

Answer: A. RANT is a dictionary word. The other strings are not.

Practice 3
Date 1: 05-10-2026. Date 2: 05-11-2026. They differ in the

  1. day
  2. month
  3. year
  4. century
Show answer

Answer: B. The month changes from 10 to 11. Day and year match.

Practice 4
A teammate has just missed an easy point and is silent. The response that best shows emotional intelligence is

  1. Laugh and retell the miss
  2. Ignore the person for the rest of the day
  3. Say the miss is done, and ask what they want to try next
  4. Announce the miss to the whole room
Show answer

Answer: C. The response notices the moment without humiliating the person, and it moves to a useful next step.

Practice 5
If × means ÷ and ÷ means ×, then 12 × 4 ÷ 2 equals

  1. 6
  2. 24
  3. 1.5
  4. 96
Show answer

Answer: A. It becomes 12 ÷ 4 × 2 = 6.

Practice 6
If P means +, Q means − and R means ×, then 8 R 2 P 6 Q 4 equals

  1. 22
  2. 14
  3. 18
  4. 20
Show answer

Answer: C. 8 × 2 + 6 − 4 = 18.

Practice 7
Which word can be made from the letters of EXAMINATION?

  1. MOTION
  2. NATION
  3. STATION
  4. TAXING
Show answer

Answer: B. MOTION needs two O’s, STATION needs S, TAXING needs G.

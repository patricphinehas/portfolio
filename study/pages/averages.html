Averages

The average is the total divided by how many numbers you added. SSC almost never asks you to add a long list. It asks what happens to the total when one person joins, one value is corrected, or two groups are combined.

Keep the sum in your working. The average is only the last step.

What SSC lists

  • Simple average of numbers.
  • Finding a missing value when the average is given.
  • Combined average of two groups.
  • A wrong reading replaced by the correct reading.

Rules and formulas

Definition

Average = sum / count.
Sum = average × count.

A new value

If the average of n values is A, and one more value x makes the average B, then x = (n+1)B − nA.

Correction

If a wrong value w was used instead of the right value r, correct sum = old sum − w + r.

Two groups

Combined average = (n1×A1 + n2×A2) / (n1 + n2).

Quick methods

Equally spaced list: average = (first + last)/2.
First n natural numbers: average (n+1)/2. First n odd numbers: average n. First n even numbers: average n + 1.
Deviation method: choose a round base, average the deviations from it, add back.
Add k to every value → the average rises by k. Multiply every value by k → the average is multiplied by k.
Overlapping groups: middle value = (sum of first group + sum of last group) − total sum.

Common traps

The average of averages is wrong when the groups have different sizes.

When one person joins a group of 5, the new count is 6, not 5.

A corrected value changes the sum by (right − wrong), then you divide by the same count.

Average speed is not an ordinary average when the distances or times differ. That case is on the time and distance page.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
The average of five numbers is 18. Their sum is

  1. 80
  2. 85
  3. 90
  4. 108

Solution

  1. Sum = average × count.
  2. 18 × 5 = 90.

Answer: C. This is the step inside almost every later average question.

Example 2
The average of four numbers is 15. A fifth number is included and the average becomes 18. The fifth number is

  1. 24
  2. 27
  3. 30
  4. 36

Solution

  1. Sum of the first four = 4 × 15 = 60.
  2. Sum of five numbers = 5 × 18 = 90.
  3. Fifth number = 90 − 60 = 30.

Answer: C. The newcomer pulls a sum of 60 up to 90.

Example 3
The average age of 5 students is 12 years. A teacher joins and the average becomes 16 years. The teacher’s age is

  1. 28
  2. 32
  3. 36
  4. 40

Solution

  1. Students’ total age = 5 × 12 = 60.
  2. New total = 6 × 16 = 96.
  3. Teacher’s age = 96 − 60 = 36.

Answer: C. The divisor becomes 6 because the teacher is the sixth person.

Example 4
The average of 10 numbers is 20. One number was read as 21 instead of 12. The correct average is

  1. 18.9
  2. 19.1
  3. 19.5
  4. 20.9

Solution

  1. Wrong sum = 10 × 20 = 200.
  2. Correct sum = 200 − 21 + 12 = 191.
  3. Correct average = 191/10 = 19.1.

Answer: B. The sum was 9 too high, so the average was 0.9 too high.

Example 5
Three tests have an average of 70 and two other tests have an average of 85. The average of all five tests is

  1. 74
  2. 76
  3. 78
  4. 80

Solution

  1. Sum = 3 × 70 + 2 × 85 = 210 + 170 = 380.
  2. Average = 380/5 = 76.

Answer: B. Do not average 70 and 85 directly. The groups are different sizes.

  1. 64
  2. 65

  4. 68

Answer: C. Total marks are 1200 + 2100 = 3300. Average is 3300/50 = 66.

Practice 6
The average of 47, 52, 55 and 46 is

  1. 49
  2. 50
  3. 51
  4. 52
Show answer

Answer: B. Deviations from 50: −3, +2, +5, −4. They add to 0, so the average is 50.

Practice 7
The average of 5 numbers is 30. Each number is increased by 4. The new average is

  1. 30
  2. 34
  3. 38
  4. 50

Answer: B. Adding 4 to every value adds 4 to the average.

Practice 8

  1. 10
  2. 9

  4. 20
Show answer

Answer: A. The average of the first n odd numbers is n.

Practice 9

  1. 50
  2. 52
  3. 56

Answer: C. 6 × 49 + 6 × 52 − 11 × 50 = 294 + 312 − 550 = 56.

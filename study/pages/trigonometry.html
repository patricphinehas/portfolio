Trigonometry and heights

Trigonometry here is the right-triangle ratios, the standard angles, and a height or a distance read from one triangle. The identities are the three Pythagorean ones, plus complementary angles.

Draw the triangle. The angle of elevation is the angle at your eye, between the ground and the line to the top.

What SSC lists

  • Trigonometric ratios of an acute angle.
  • Standard values at 0°, 30°, 45°, 60° and 90°, used carefully at 90°.
  • Complementary angles and the standard identities.
  • Heights and distances with one or two right triangles.

Rules and formulas

Ratios

In a right triangle, sin θ = opposite / hypotenuse.
cos θ = adjacent / hypotenuse.
tan θ = opposite / adjacent.
cosec, sec and cot are the reciprocals of sin, cos and tan.

Standard values

sin 0 = 0, sin 30 = 1/2, sin 45 = 1/√2, sin 60 = √3/2, sin 90 = 1.
cos 0 = 1, cos 30 = √3/2, cos 45 = 1/√2, cos 60 = 1/2, cos 90 = 0.
tan 45 = 1. tan 90 is not defined.

Identities

sin²θ + cos²θ = 1.
1 + tan²θ = sec²θ.
1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ.
sin(90° − θ) = cos θ. cos(90° − θ) = sin θ. tan(90° − θ) = cot θ.

Height

If the angle of elevation is θ and the distance along the ground is d, height = d × tan θ.
If θ = 45°, height equals the ground distance.

Quick methods

Memory row: sin 0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90° = √0/2, √1/2, √2/2, √3/2, √4/2. cos is the same row reversed.
(sec θ + tan θ)(sec θ − tan θ) = 1, and (cosec θ + cot θ)(cosec θ − cot θ) = 1.
For acute angles, sin A = cos B or tan A × tan B = 1 means A + B = 90°.
sin 2θ = 2 sin θ cos θ.
Shadow = height → elevation 45°. Shadow = √3 × height → 30°. Height = √3 × shadow → 60°.

Complementary angles and ratios

sin(90° − θ) = cos θ: rewrite so both terms use one angle, then cancel.
If tan θ is given, divide top and bottom by cos θ: (a sin θ + b cos θ)/(c sin θ + d cos θ) = (a tan θ + b)/(c tan θ + d).
In a cos²θ + b sin²θ = c, replace cos²θ by 1 − sin²θ.

Common traps

tan 90° is undefined. Do not write it as 1/0 and then use 0 in a larger expression.

sin²θ + cos²θ is 1. It is not equal to tan²θ.

Complementary angles add to 90°, not to 180°.

In a two-point height question, the farther point has the smaller angle. Set the nearer distance as the unknown that matches tan 45° if that angle is 45°.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
The value of sin 30° is

  1. 1/√2
  2. √3/2
  3. 1/2
  4. 1

Solution

  1. sin 30° is the opposite-over-hypotenuse in a 30-60-90 triangle, which is half the hypotenuse.
  2. The standard value is 1/2.

Answer: C. sin 60° is √3/2, and that swap is the common error.

Example 2
If sin θ = 3/5 and θ is acute, then tan θ is

  1. 3/4
  2. 4/5
  3. 4/3
  4. 5/3

Solution

  1. A right triangle with opposite 3 and hypotenuse 5 has adjacent √(25−9) = 4.
  2. tan θ = opposite / adjacent = 3/4.
  3. cos θ = 4/5, which is not what was asked.

Answer: A. The triple is 3-4-5.

Example 3
The value of tan 45° + cot 45° is

  1. 0
  2. 1
  3. 2
  4. √2

Solution

  1. tan 45° = 1 and cot 45° = 1.
  2. Sum = 2.

Answer: C. Each ratio equals 1 because opposite and adjacent are equal.

Example 4
From a point on level ground 20 m from the foot of a tower, the angle of elevation of the top is 45°. The height of the tower is

  1. 10 m
  2. 20 m
  3. 20√3 m
  4. 40 m

Solution

  1. tan 45° = 1 = height / 20.
  2. Height = 20 m.

Answer: B. At 45° the opposite and adjacent sides are equal.

Example 5
The angle of elevation of a tower is 45° from a point on the ground, and 30° from a point 20 m farther away. The height of the tower is

  1. 10(√3 − 1) m
  2. 10(√3 + 1) m
  3. 20(√3 + 1) m
  4. 20 m

Solution

  1. From the nearer point, tan 45° = 1, so the nearer distance equals the height h.
  2. From the farther point, tan 30° = 1/√3 = h / (h + 20).
  3. √3 h = h + 20, so h(√3 − 1) = 20.
  4. h = 20/(√3 − 1) = 10(√3 + 1) after rationalising.

Answer: B. Multiply numerator and denominator by √3 + 1. The denominator becomes 2, so the 20 becomes 10.

Example 6
(CGL pattern) (4 sin 55° + 2 cos 35°) / (3 cos 35° + 3 sin 55°) equals

  1. 1
  2. 2
  3. 1/2
  4. 6

Solution

  1. sin 55° = cos 35°.
  2. Numerator = 6 cos 35°. Denominator = 6 cos 35°.
  3. Value = 1.

Answer: A. No table value is needed once the angles are made equal.

Example 7
(CGL pattern) If 5 tan θ = 4 and θ is acute, then (5 sin θ − 3 cos θ)/(5 sin θ + 2 cos θ) is

  1. 1/3
  2. 1/6
  3. 2/7
  4. 1/2

Solution

  1. Divide by cos θ: (5 tan θ − 3)/(5 tan θ + 2).
  2. 5 tan θ = 4, so (4 − 3)/(4 + 2) = 1/6.

Answer: B. Finding sin θ and cos θ separately works but takes longer.

Example 8
(CGL pattern) If 3 cos²θ + 5 sin²θ = 4 and 0° < θ < 90°, then θ is

  1. 30°
  2. 45°
  3. 60°
  4. 90°

Solution

  1. 3(1 − sin²θ) + 5 sin²θ = 4.
  2. 3 + 2 sin²θ = 4, so sin²θ = 1/2.
  3. θ = 45°.

Answer: B. 90° is excluded by the range.

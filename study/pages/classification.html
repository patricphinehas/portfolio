Reasoning · Tier-I and Paper-I

Classification

Classification asks which item does not belong. Three options share a rule. One option breaks it. The rule can be meaning, letters, or numbers.

Find a rule that fits three options exactly. If your rule fits all four, it is not the rule.

What SSC lists

  • Semantic classification: which word is unlike the others.
  • Symbolic and number classification.
  • Figural classification: three figures share a rotation, a side-count, or a shading.
  • The odd item is the answer, not the group that remains.

Rules and formulas

Meaning

Group by a clear class: prime, metal, male relative, polygon, capital city.

Number

Check squares, cubes, primes, multiples, and a constant difference.

Letters

Check vowels, letter-gap, or whether the word is formed by +1 letters.

Figures

Count sides, count lines, or see whether the figure is rotated or reflected.

Common traps

A rule that fits every option does not identify the odd one.

9 is odd, but in the list 2, 3, 5, 9 the stronger break is that 9 is not prime.

Do not use a private association (“I have seen this word in biology”) if a cleaner class exists.

The answer is the outsider, so read the question if it instead asks for the one that belongs.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
Which is the odd one out?

  1. 2
  2. 3
  3. 5
  4. 9

Solution

  1. 2, 3 and 5 are prime.
  2. 9 = 3 × 3, so it is not prime.

Answer: D. Odd-versus-even does not work: 2 is even, so it would break as well. Primality isolates exactly one option.

Example 2
Which is the odd one out?

  1. triangle
  2. square
  3. pentagon
  4. circle

Solution

  1. A triangle, a square and a pentagon are polygons with straight sides.
  2. A circle has no straight side.

Answer: D. The first three are named by the number of sides.

Example 3
Which is the odd number?

  1. 16
  2. 25
  3. 36
  4. 48

Solution

  1. 16, 25 and 36 are squares: 4², 5² and 6².
  2. 48 is not a square.

Answer: D. The next square after 36 would have been 49, not 48.

Example 4
Which word does not belong?

  1. iron
  2. copper
  3. mercury
  4. gold

Solution

  1. All four are metals.
  2. Mercury is a liquid at ordinary room temperature. The other three are solids.

Answer: C. If the paper uses a figure, the same idea applies: three share a property and one does not.

Example 5
(CGL pattern) Three of the following are alike. Which one is different?

  1. EJM
  2. DIL
  3. CHL
  4. FKN

Solution

  1. E→J is +5 and J→M is +3. D→I +5, I→L +3. F→K +5, K→N +3.
  2. C→H is +5 but H→L is +4.

Answer: C. Write the letter gaps under each cluster.

Example 6
(CGL pattern) Three of the following number sets are alike. Which one is different?

  1. 7 – 49 – 56
  2. 5 – 25 – 30
  3. 6 – 36 – 42
  4. 4 – 16 – 24

Solution

  1. The pattern is a, a², a² + a.
  2. 4, 16 would need 20, not 24.

Answer: D. Operate on the whole numbers, not on their digits.

Example 7
(CGL pattern) Three of the four figures are alike. Which one does not belong?

Solution

  1. A, B and D are the same figure turned by 0°, 90° and 270°.
  2. C is a mirror image of the figure. No rotation can turn the figure into its mirror image.

Answer: C. Check which way the bars point relative to the long stroke and the dot.

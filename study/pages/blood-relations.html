Reasoning · Tier-I and Paper-I

Blood relations

A blood-relation puzzle describes a family in one or two sentences. You translate each phrase into a generation and a gender. The notice calls this relationship concepts. The usual form is a person pointing at someone and describing the link.

Do not decide gender from a name. Use only the words brother, sister, mother, father, son and daughter.

What SSC lists

  • Relationship concepts and family descriptions.
  • One-sentence “pointing to” questions.
  • A chain of two or three relations.
  • The idea carries into seating puzzles when a family also sits in a row.

Rules and formulas

Generation

Father or mother: one generation up. Son or daughter: one generation down.
Brother or sister: the same generation.

Partner words

A mother’s brother is an uncle. A father’s sister is an aunt.
A brother’s son is a nephew. A brother’s daughter is a niece.
A mother’s mother is a maternal grandmother.

Only child

“The only son of my grandfather” is your father, if your grandfather has one son.
“The only daughter of my mother” is the woman herself, if she is that only daughter.

Drawing

A small sketch helps: a square or just the letter for each person, with a line up for parent and a line across for brother or sister. Gender goes on the sketch only when the sentence gives it.

Common traps

A name does not tell you the gender.

“Sibling of my parent” is an uncle or aunt, not a cousin.

The person speaking is in the family. Do not leave the speaker out of the sketch.

“Only son” and “only daughter” remove the extra branches. Read the word only.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
Pointing to a woman, Raj says, “She is the daughter of the only son of my grandfather.” How is the woman related to Raj?

  1. Mother
  2. Sister
  3. Aunt
  4. Daughter

Solution

  1. The only son of Raj’s grandfather is Raj’s father.
  2. The woman is that man’s daughter.
  3. She is Raj’s sister.

Answer: B. The standard reading takes the paternal grandfather, whose only son is Raj’s father. Daughter would need Raj to be that son, and he cannot be his own grandfather’s son.

Example 2
A is the brother of B. B is the sister of C. A is C’s

  1. brother
  2. sister
  3. father
  4. uncle

Solution

  1. A is male, because A is a brother.
  2. B and C are siblings, so A is also a sibling of C.
  3. A is C’s brother. C’s gender is not required.

Answer: A. The sentence never says whether C is male or female, and it does not need to.

Example 3
A woman says of a boy, “His mother is the only daughter of my mother.” The boy is the woman’s

  1. brother
  2. nephew
  3. son
  4. uncle

Solution

  1. The only daughter of the woman’s mother is the woman herself.
  2. The boy’s mother is the woman.
  3. The boy is her son.

Answer: C. There is no extra sister in the sentence, because of the word only.

Example 4
P is the father of Q. Q is the mother of R. P is R’s

  1. father
  2. maternal grandfather
  3. maternal uncle
  4. brother

Solution

  1. Q is R’s mother, so the link is on the mother’s side.
  2. P is Q’s father.
  3. P is R’s maternal grandfather.

Answer: B. A paternal grandfather would be the father’s father. Here the parent in the middle is the mother.

Example 5
(CGL pattern) A + B means A is the mother of B; A − B means A is the brother of B; A × B means A is the wife of B; A ÷ B means A is the father of B. In P − Q × R ÷ S, how is P related to S?

  1. Mother’s brother
  2. Father’s brother
  3. Father
  4. Grandfather

Solution

  1. P is Q’s brother. Q is R’s wife. R is S’s father.
  2. So Q is S’s mother, and P is her brother.

Answer: A. Decode one symbol at a time and draw the chain.

English · Tier-I and Tier-II English

Direct and indirect speech

Direct speech quotes the words. Indirect speech reports them. If the reporting verb is in the past, the tense inside the quote usually steps one stage back. A universal truth does not step back.

Also change the pointer words: today becomes that day, tomorrow becomes the next day, now becomes then.

What SSC lists

  • Conversion into direct and indirect narration.
  • Statements, questions and requests.
  • The tense shift after a past reporting verb.
  • Words that change: today, tomorrow, yesterday, this, now, ago.

Rules and formulas

Statements

Said that… is the usual frame.
Said to someone becomes told someone.
Present becomes past: am → was, is writing → was writing, has done → had done, will → would.

Pointer words

Today → that day. Tomorrow → the next day. Yesterday → the previous day.
Now → then. This → that. Ago → before. Here → there.

Questions

Said to becomes asked.
A yes/no question takes if or whether, and the question order becomes a statement order.
A wh-question keeps the wh-word and also becomes a statement order. The tense still shifts.

No shift

If the reported words are a universal truth or a habit that is still true, the tense can stay: The teacher said that the earth revolves around the sun.
A request: said, “Please…” → requested someone to…

Common traps

Do not keep the question mark in indirect speech.

Will becomes would. It does not stay will after said.

Tomorrow does not stay tomorrow after a past reporting verb.

Told is not followed by to before the person: told me, not told to me, in the standard form. Said to me is the direct-side pattern.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
She said, “I am happy.” The indirect form is

  1. She said that she is happy.
  2. She said that she was happy.
  3. She said that I was happy.
  4. She told that she is happy.

Solution

  1. Said is past, so am becomes was.
  2. I becomes she, because she is reporting her own words.
  3. She said that she was happy.

Answer: B. The pronoun follows the speaker, not the word I printed in the quote.

Example 2
He said, “I will go tomorrow.” The indirect form is

  1. He said that he will go tomorrow.
  2. He said that he would go the next day.
  3. He said that he would go yesterday.
  4. He told that I will go the next day.

Solution

  1. Will becomes would.
  2. Tomorrow becomes the next day.
  3. I becomes he.

Answer: B. Both the modal and the time word have to change.

Example 3
The teacher said, “The earth revolves around the sun.” The indirect form is

  1. The teacher said that the earth revolved around the sun.
  2. The teacher said that the earth revolves around the sun.
  3. The teacher said that the earth had revolved around the sun.
  4. The teacher told that the earth revolved.

Solution

  1. This is treated as a universal truth.
  2. The present tense stays.
  3. The teacher said that the earth revolves around the sun.

Answer: B. A past shift here would suggest that the fact itself has expired.

Example 4
He said to me, “Where do you live?” The indirect form is

  1. He asked me where I lived.
  2. He asked me where do I live.
  3. He said to me where I live.
  4. He told me where did I live.

Solution

  1. Said to me becomes asked me.
  2. The question order becomes a statement: where I lived.
  3. Do you live, present, becomes lived.

Answer: A. The auxiliary do disappears once the sentence is no longer a direct question.

Example 5
She said to him, “Please help me.” The indirect form is

  1. She said him to help her.
  2. She requested him to help her.
  3. She asked him that help her.
  4. She told him please help her.

Solution

  1. Please marks a request.
  2. Said to him becomes requested him.
  3. Help me becomes to help her.

Answer: B. The pronoun me changes because she is no longer speaking directly.

Quantitative Aptitude · Tier-I and Paper-I

Ratio and proportion

A ratio compares two quantities in the same units. 2:3 means 2 parts against 3 parts, or the fraction 2/3. Proportion says two ratios are equal.

SSC uses ratios to split money, mix partners, and rewrite a word problem before any percentage or average work. If the units differ, convert them first.

What SSC lists

  • Ratio and proportion, including a continued ratio a:b:c.
  • Dividing a quantity in a given ratio.
  • Finding an unknown when two ratios are linked by a common term.
  • Simple use of componendo and dividendo when the question is already in that form.

Rules and formulas

Meaning

a:b = a/b. The two parts of a whole split in a:b are a/(a+b) and b/(a+b) of the whole.

Combining

If A:B = m:n and B:C = p:q, make the B-parts match.
A:B:C = (m×p) : (n×p) : (n×q) only after n and p are the same B-term. Scale first.

Componendo and dividendo

If a/b = c/d, then (a+b)/(a−b) = (c+d)/(c−d), provided the denominators are not zero.
If (a+b)/(a−b) = p/q, then a/b = (p+q)/(p−q).

Units

A ratio has no unit. 2 hours : 30 minutes is 120:30 = 4:1, not 2:30.

Quick methods

Mean proportional of a and b = √(ab). Third proportional to a and b = b²/a. Fourth proportional to a, b, c = bc/a.
If 2A = 3B = 4C, set each equal to the LCM (12): A = 6, B = 4, C = 3.
If a split a:b (a > b) gives the first person Rs d more, one part = d/(a − b).
Share of A in a total T split a:b = aT/(a + b).

Common traps

Do not add ratios of different pairs until the shared term is scaled to the same number.

2:3 of Rs 150 is not Rs 2 and Rs 3. One part is 150/5 = 30.

A ratio written with mixed units is wrong until the units match.

If each term of a ratio is increased by the same number, the ratio usually changes.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
Rs 150 is divided in the ratio 2:3. The larger share is

  1. 50
  2. 60
  3. 90
  4. 100

Solution

  1. Total parts = 2 + 3 = 5.
  2. One part = 150/5 = 30.
  3. Larger share = 3 × 30 = 90.

Answer: C. The smaller share is 60, and 60 + 90 = 150.

Example 2
A:B = 2:3 and B:C = 4:5. Then A:B:C is

  1. 2:3:5
  2. 8:12:15
  3. 2:4:5
  4. 6:8:15

Solution

  1. B is 3 in the first ratio and 4 in the second.
  2. Scale the first ratio by 4 and the second by 3: A:B = 8:12 and B:C = 12:15.
  3. A:B:C = 8:12:15.

Answer: B. The common B-term must be 12, not left as 3 and 4.

Example 3
Two numbers are in the ratio 3:5. If each is increased by 10, the ratio becomes 5:7. The smaller number is

  1. 10
  2. 15
  3. 20
  4. 25

Solution

  1. Let the numbers be 3x and 5x.
  2. (3x+10)/(5x+10) = 5/7.
  3. 7(3x+10) = 5(5x+10) gives 21x + 70 = 25x + 50, so x = 5.
  4. Smaller number = 15.

Answer: B. The numbers are 15 and 25. After adding 10 they are 25 and 35, which is 5:7.

Example 4
If (a+b)/(a−b) = 7/3, then a/b is

  1. 5/2
  2. 2/5
  3. 7/3
  4. 3/2

Solution

  1. By the inverse of componendo and dividendo, a/b = (7+3)/(7−3).
  2. a/b = 10/4 = 5/2.
  3. Check: a = 5k, b = 2k gives (7k)/(3k) = 7/3.

Answer: A. Cross-multiplying 3(a+b) = 7(a−b) leads to the same 5/2.

Example 5
A sum of Rs 350 is split in the ratio A:B:C = 8:12:15. A’s share is

  1. 70
  2. 80
  3. 120
  4. 150

Solution

  1. Total parts = 8 + 12 + 15 = 35.
  2. One part = 350/35 = 10.
  3. A’s share = 8 × 10 = 80.

Answer: B. B gets 120 and C gets 150.

Example 6
(CGL pattern) A bag has ₹5, ₹2 and ₹1 coins in the ratio 2:3:5 by number. The total value is ₹420. The number of ₹2 coins is

  1. 40
  2. 60
  3. 100
  4. 20

Solution

  1. One set of 2 + 3 + 5 coins is worth 2×5 + 3×2 + 5×1 = ₹21.
  2. 420 / 21 = 20 sets.
  3. ₹2 coins = 3 × 20 = 60.

Answer: B. Convert the count ratio into a value ratio before dividing the money.

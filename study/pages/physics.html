Physics for General Awareness

The physics in General Awareness is school science: units, a few laws, and the difference between heat and temperature or between a mirror and a lens. Numerical problems of the kind in the maths paper are rare here. A one-line law and its SI unit are the usual prize.

Skip derivations. If you can state the law and the unit, you can attempt the item.

What SSC lists

  • Scientific research and everyday science, the physics slice.
  • SI units, motion, force, work and electricity.
  • Light and sound at a descriptive level.
  • The same facts support some current-affairs science items when a mission or a unit is in the news.

Rules and formulas

Units

Force: newton. Energy and work: joule. Power: watt. Pressure: pascal. Frequency: hertz. Electric charge: coulomb. Current: ampere. Resistance: ohm.
The approximate value of g used in school is 9.8 m/s². The speed of light in vacuum is about 3 × 10⁸ m/s.

Motion and force

Speed is distance over time. Velocity has direction. Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity.
Newton’s first law: a body keeps its state unless a net force acts. Second: F = ma. Third: every action has an equal and opposite reaction.

Electricity

Ohm’s law: V = IR, for a given conductor under the conditions where the law holds.
Power in a simple circuit can be written VI.

Light and sound

Sound needs a material medium. Light does not.
A concave mirror converges parallel rays. A convex mirror diverges them.
A convex lens converges. A concave lens diverges.

Common traps

The unit of power is the watt, not the joule. The joule is work or energy.

Sound does not travel through a vacuum. Light does.

Concave and convex do opposite jobs for mirrors and the everyday description of lenses. Learn them as a pair, not as one word “curved”.

g is about 9.8 m/s², not 9.8 km/s.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
The SI unit of force is the

  1. joule
  2. watt
  3. newton
  4. pascal

Solution

  1. Force is the newton, from F = ma.
  2. The joule is energy, the watt is power, and the pascal is pressure.

Answer: C. A newton is the force that gives a mass of 1 kg an acceleration of 1 m/s².

Example 2
Ohm’s law relates

  1. force, mass and acceleration
  2. voltage, current and resistance
  3. mass and energy
  4. frequency and wavelength only

Solution

  1. V = IR.
  2. Voltage, current and resistance.

Answer: B. F = ma is Newton’s second law, the neighbouring trap.

Example 3
Sound waves

  1. travel through a vacuum
  2. need a material medium
  3. are the same thing as light
  4. have no speed

Solution

  1. Sound is a mechanical wave.
  2. It needs a medium. Light does not.

Answer: B. That is why the school demonstration is a bell in a jar with the air removed.

Example 4
A convex lens

  1. diverges parallel rays
  2. converges parallel rays
  3. is always used only as a car mirror
  4. blocks all light

Solution

  1. A convex lens is thicker in the middle.
  2. It converges parallel rays toward a focus.

Answer: B. A concave lens is the diverging one. A car’s side mirror is usually a convex mirror, which is a different object.

  1. ampere
  2. ohm
  3. coulomb
  4. volt
Show answer

Answer: C. Charge is the coulomb. Current is the ampere.

Practice 3
Newton’s third law says that

  1. F = ma
  2. a body at rest has no mass
  3. action and reaction are equal and opposite
  4. energy is always lost
Show answer

Answer: C. The two forces are on different bodies, equal in size and opposite in direction.

Practice 4
A concave mirror

  1. always diverges parallel rays
  2. converges parallel rays
  3. is a lens
  4. produces sound
Show answer

Answer: B. Parallel rays reflect toward the focus of a concave mirror.

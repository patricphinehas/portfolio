Quantitative Aptitude · Tier-I and Paper-I

Geometry

The geometry in this syllabus is the geometry of the Class 10 textbook: triangles, their centres, similarity, and the basic circle theorems. You are not asked to write proofs. You are asked to use one fact on a figure.

Name the centre before you use it. Centroid, incentre, circumcentre and orthocentre are different points.

What SSC lists

  • Triangles and the four classical centres.
  • Congruence and similarity of triangles.
  • Circle, chord, tangent, and the angle subtended by an arc.
  • Common tangents at the level of equal tangents from a point, not long constructions.

Rules and formulas

Triangle

Angles of a triangle add to 180°.
An exterior angle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles.
Pythagoras: in a right triangle, a² + b² = c².

Centres

Centroid: medians meet, and they cut each other in 2:1, longer part toward the vertex.
Incentre: angle bisectors meet. Inradius = Area / s, where s is the semi-perimeter.
Circumcentre: perpendicular bisectors meet. In a right triangle it is the midpoint of the hypotenuse, and R = hypotenuse / 2.
Orthocentre: altitudes meet. In a right triangle it is the vertex of the right angle.

Similarity

Triangles are similar when two angles match, or all three pairs of sides are in proportion.
Ratio of areas = square of the ratio of corresponding sides.

Circle

The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference standing on the same arc.
The angle in a semicircle is 90°.
A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact.
Tangents from an external point are equal. Length of a tangent = √(d² − r²), where d is the distance from the centre to the external point.

Quick methods

Polygon with n sides: interior angles add to (n − 2) × 180°. Each exterior angle of a regular polygon = 360°/n.
Diagonals = n(n − 3)/2.
Equilateral triangle of side a: height (√3/2)a, inradius a/(2√3), circumradius a/√3, so R = 2r.
Angle bisector of A cuts BC in the ratio AB : AC.
Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180°.

Two circles and tangents

Centres d apart, radii r₁ and r₂.
Direct (external) common tangent = √[d² − (r₁ − r₂)²].
Transverse (internal) common tangent = √[d² − (r₁ + r₂)²].
If both are given: (direct)² − (transverse)² = 4r₁r₂.

Chords

The perpendicular from the centre bisects a chord.
(half chord)² + (distance from centre)² = r².
Equal chords are equidistant from the centre.

Common traps

The centroid divides a median in 2:1, not in 1:1.

In a right triangle the circumcentre is the midpoint of the hypotenuse, not a vertex. The orthocentre is the right-angled vertex.

Similar triangles have proportional sides. Congruent triangles have equal sides. Area scales with the square of the side ratio.

A radius meets a tangent at 90°. It does not bisect a chord unless that chord fact is separately given.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
The angles of a triangle are in the ratio 2:3:4. The largest angle is

  1. 40°
  2. 60°
  3. 80°
  4. 90°

Solution

  1. The parts add to 9, and 9 parts = 180°.
  2. One part = 20°.
  3. Largest angle = 4 × 20° = 80°.

Answer: C. The angles are 40°, 60° and 80°, which do add to 180°.

Example 2
The legs of a right triangle are 6 cm and 8 cm. Its inradius is

  1. 2 cm
  2. 2.5 cm
  3. 4 cm
  4. 5 cm

Solution

  1. Hypotenuse = √(36+64) = 10 cm.
  2. For a right triangle, inradius = (a + b − c) / 2.
  3. (6 + 8 − 10) / 2 = 2 cm.

Answer: A. Area is 24 and semi-perimeter is 12, so r = Area/s = 2. The circumradius is 5 cm, which is the other common option.

Example 3
The area of an equilateral triangle of side 4 cm is

  1. 4√3 cm²
  2. √3 cm²
  3. 8√3 cm²
  4. 16√3 cm²

Solution

  1. Area = (√3 / 4) × side².
  2. (√3 / 4) × 16 = 4√3.

Answer: A. The height is (√3 / 2) × 4 = 2√3, and (1/2) × 4 × 2√3 gives the same area.

Example 4
A triangle is drawn with the diameter of a semicircle as one side, and the third vertex on the semicircle. The angle at that third vertex is

  1. 45°
  2. 60°
  3. 90°
  4. 120°

Solution

  1. The angle in a semicircle, standing on the diameter, is a right angle.
  2. So the angle at the circumference is 90°.

Answer: C. The two base angles depend on where the vertex sits. Their sum is 90°, but the angle on the diameter is fixed.

Example 5
A tangent of length 12 cm is drawn from a point 13 cm from the centre of a circle. The radius is

  1. 4 cm
  2. 5 cm
  3. 6 cm
  4. 8 cm

Solution

  1. Radius is perpendicular to the tangent, so the triangle is right-angled.
  2. r = √(13² − 12²) = √(169 − 144) = √25 = 5 cm.

Answer: B. This is the 5-12-13 triangle.

Example 6
(CGL pattern) Two circles of radii 4 cm and 8 cm have centres 20 cm apart. The transverse common tangent is

  1. 12 cm
  2. 16 cm
  3. 18 cm
  4. √304 cm

Solution

  1. Transverse = √[d² − (r₁ + r₂)²].
  2. √(400 − 144) = √256 = 16.

Answer: B. √304 is what you get by using (r₁ − r₂), which is the direct tangent.

Example 7
(CGL pattern) The direct common tangent of two circles is 13 and the transverse common tangent is 5. The product of the radii is

  1. 36
  2. 18
  3. 144
  4. 9

Solution

  1. (direct)² − (transverse)² = 4r₁r₂.
  2. 169 − 25 = 144 = 4r₁r₂, so r₁r₂ = 36.

Answer: A. 144 is 4r₁r₂, not the product.

Example 8
(CGL pattern) Parallel chords AB = 16 cm and CD = 30 cm lie on opposite sides of the centre, 23 cm apart. The diameter is

  1. 30 cm
  2. 34 cm
  3. 17 cm
  4. 40 cm

Solution

  1. Let the distances from the centre be a and 23 − a.
  2. 8² + a² = 15² + (23 − a)², so 46a = 690 and a = 15.
  3. r² = 64 + 225 = 289, r = 17, diameter 34.

Answer: B. Check the other side: 15² + 8² = 289 as well.

Example 9
(CGL pattern) ΔABC ≅ ΔPQR. ∠B = 70°, ∠Q = (4y + 10)°, BC = 20 and QR = 2x + y. Then x + y is

  1. 17.5
  2. 15
  3. 20
  4. 12.5

Solution

  1. Corresponding parts are equal: 4y + 10 = 70, so y = 15.
  2. 2x + 15 = 20, so x = 2.5.
  3. x + y = 17.5.

Answer: A. Match the vertices in the order written: B with Q, C with R.

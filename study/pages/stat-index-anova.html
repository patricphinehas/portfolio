ANOVA, time series and index numbers

The last block of Paper-II is three tools that each have a standard setup. ANOVA compares means of several groups. A time series is a variable recorded in time order. An index number is a single figure that stands for the change in a group of prices or quantities.

Fisher’s index is the one called ideal, because it meets the time-reversal and factor-reversal tests. Laspeyres and Paasche fail both tests.

What SSC lists

  • One-way and two-way analysis of variance.
  • Components of a time series, trend and seasonal variation.
  • Index numbers: Laspeyres, Paasche, Fisher, base shifting and splicing.
  • A cost of living index as a weighted price index of a consumption basket.

Rules and formulas

ANOVA

One-way ANOVA compares means of several groups classified by one factor.
Two-way ANOVA has two factors.
The test statistic is an F ratio: variation between groups relative to variation within groups.
A large F is evidence that the group means are not all equal, if the model assumptions hold.

Time series

The four components are trend, seasonal variation, cyclical variation and irregular variation.
A moving average smooths a series to show the trend. A short average follows the series more closely. A long average smooths more.
Seasonal variation repeats within the year: a festival month, a harvest.

Index numbers

A simple aggregative price index is (ΣP1 / ΣP0) × 100.
Laspeyres uses base-year quantities: (Σ P1 Q0 / Σ P0 Q0) × 100.
Paasche uses current quantities: (Σ P1 Q1 / Σ P0 Q1) × 100.
Fisher’s index is the square root of the product of Laspeyres and Paasche.

Using an index

Base shifting: new index = (old index / old index in the new base period) × 100.
Splicing joins a series onto an older series when the basket changes.
A cost of living index applies weights from a household budget to price relatives. It is a consumer price index in spirit.

Quick methods

Price relative = P₁/P₀ × 100.
Time reversal: P₀₁ × P₁₀ = 1. Factor reversal: P₀₁ × Q₀₁ = ΣP₁Q₁/ΣP₀Q₀.
One-way ANOVA df: between k − 1, within N − k, total N − 1.
F = MSB/MSW, each mean square = sum of squares / its df.
Real (deflated) value = money value / price index × 100.

Common traps

Laspeyres holds quantities at the base year. Paasche uses the current year. Swapping them is the standard trap.

Fisher’s index is the geometric mean of the other two, which is the square root of the product, not the ordinary average.

ANOVA compares means. It is not a correlation coefficient.

A moving average of an even number of terms needs a centering step if you want the average to sit on a real time point. A 3-term average already sits on the middle term.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
Laspeyres’ price index uses quantities of

  1. the current year
  2. the base year
  3. a future year
  4. no year

Solution

  1. The weights are Q0.
  2. That is the base period.

Answer: B. Paasche is the one that uses Q1.

Example 2
If Laspeyres is 125 and Paasche is 80, Fisher’s index is

  1. 100
  2. 102.5
  3. 205
  4. 45

Solution

  1. Fisher = √(125 × 80) = √10000 = 100.
  2. The ordinary average would be 102.5, which is the trap.

Answer: A. The square root of the product is the geometric mean.

Example 3
The four components of a time series include

  1. mean, median, mode and range
  2. trend, seasonal, cyclical and irregular
  3. Type I and Type II only
  4. LAN and WAN

Solution

  1. Trend is the long movement.
  2. Seasonal repeats within the year, cyclical is a longer swing, and irregular is the leftover.

Answer: B. Mean and median are not the components.

Example 4
One-way ANOVA is used to compare

  1. several group means
  2. two spelling mistakes
  3. a URL and a file
  4. the keys of a keyboard

Solution

  1. One factor divides the data into groups.
  2. The question is whether the means differ.

Answer: A. The statistic is F.

Chemistry for General Awareness

Chemistry in this paper is the language of substances: elements and compounds, acids and bases, and a few formulas you already met in Class 10. A pH question or a “which gas” question is more likely than a long equation.

Learn what the common formulas refer to, and which ion or reaction the textbook name points at.

What SSC lists

  • Everyday chemistry inside General Awareness.
  • Atoms, elements, compounds and mixtures.
  • Acids, bases, salts and pH.
  • A few formulas and separation methods.

Rules and formulas

Atoms

The atomic number is the number of protons.
The mass number is protons plus neutrons.
An element has one kind of atom. A compound has atoms of different elements chemically bonded. A mixture can be separated by physical means.

pH and indicators

pH 7 is neutral at the ordinary school standard. Below 7 is acidic. Above 7 is alkaline.
An acid turns blue litmus red. A base turns red litmus blue.

Formulas

Water H₂O. Common salt NaCl. Hydrochloric acid HCl. Sulphuric acid H₂SO₄. Sodium hydroxide NaOH. Calcium carbonate CaCO₃, the main substance in limestone and marble. Ammonia NH₃. Methane CH₄.

A few processes

Filtration separates an insoluble solid from a liquid. Distillation separates liquids by boiling point, or a dissolved solid from water by evaporation and condensation.
Group 18 of the periodic table is the noble gases, which are chemically almost inert.

Common traps

Atomic number is protons, not the mass number.

pH below 7 is acid, not alkali.

Blue litmus turning red means acid. Do not reverse the colours.

CaCO₃ is calcium carbonate. It is not CaO, which is quicklime.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
The atomic number of an element equals the number of

  1. neutrons
  2. protons
  3. neutrons plus protons
  4. electrons in the nucleus

Solution

  1. Atomic number is the proton count.
  2. Mass number is protons plus neutrons.
  3. Electrons are not in the nucleus.

Answer: B. In a neutral atom the electron count equals the proton count, but the definition of atomic number is the protons.

Example 2
A solution with pH 4 is

  1. neutral
  2. strongly alkaline
  3. acidic
  4. a noble gas

Solution

  1. 7 is neutral.
  2. 4 is below 7, so the solution is acidic.

Answer: C. A pH of 10 would have been alkaline.

Example 3
The chemical formula of common salt is

  1. NaOH
  2. NaCl
  3. HCl
  4. CaCO₃

Solution

  1. Common salt is sodium chloride.
  2. The formula is NaCl.

Answer: B. NaOH is caustic soda, HCl is hydrochloric acid, and CaCO₃ is calcium carbonate.

Example 4
Blue litmus turns red in

  1. a base
  2. an acid
  3. distilled water
  4. a noble gas

Solution

  1. Acids turn blue litmus red.
  2. Bases turn red litmus blue.

Answer: B. The colour change is tied to the paper you started with. Read which litmus the question names.

Example 5
(CGL pattern) Which of these elements is a metalloid?

  1. Iron
  2. Oxygen
  3. Sodium
  4. Silicon

Solution

  1. Metalloids have properties between metals and non-metals.
  2. Silicon, boron, germanium and arsenic are the usual examples.

Answer: D. Iron and sodium are metals; oxygen is a non-metal.

Example 6
(CGL pattern) A redox reaction is caused by

  1. transfer of electrons between reactants
  2. transfer of electrons between products
  3. transfer of neutrons between reactants
  4. exchange of halogens

Solution

  1. Oxidation is loss of electrons; reduction is gain.
  2. Both happen together between the reactants.

Answer: A. Remember OIL RIG: oxidation is loss, reduction is gain.

Quantitative Aptitude · Tier-I and Paper-I

Number systems

A number-system question is about how whole numbers, fractions and decimals are put together: factors, multiples, remainders and place value. The notice keeps this at matric level.

Square roots of perfect squares belong here. Surds that need identities are on the algebra page.

What SSC lists

  • Whole numbers, decimals and fractions.
  • Relationships between numbers: factors, multiples, HCF, LCM and remainders.
  • Square roots of perfect squares and simple decimals.
  • Divisibility tests used to reject options quickly.

Rules and formulas

HCF and LCM

For two numbers, product = HCF × LCM.
Do not use that one-line product rule for three numbers.

Divisibility

2: last digit even. 3: digit sum divisible by 3. 4: last two digits. 5: ends in 0 or 5.
8: last three digits. 9: digit sum divisible by 9. 11: alternating sum is a multiple of 11, including 0.
A number is divisible by 6 only if it is divisible by both 2 and 3.

Fractions

a/b of a quantity means multiply by a/b.
For positive fractions, a/b is larger than c/d when a×d is larger than b×c.
HCF of fractions = (HCF of numerators) / (LCM of denominators).

Square roots

For non-negative numbers, √(a×b) = √a × √b.
√0.49 = √(49/100) = 0.7. Pair decimal digits outward from the point.

Quick methods

Unit digit of a power: unit digits repeat in a cycle of at most 4. Divide the power by 4 and use the remainder; remainder 0 means the 4th term of the cycle.
Number of factors: write N = pᵃ × qᵇ × rᶜ. Factors = (a+1)(b+1)(c+1).
Sum 1 + 2 + … + n = n(n+1)/2. Sum of squares = n(n+1)(2n+1)/6. Sum of cubes = [n(n+1)/2]².
Remainder of a sum or product: take each part’s remainder first, combine, then take the remainder again.

Simplification (BODMAS)

Order: brackets, of, then ÷ and × from left to right, then + and − from left to right.
÷ and × have equal rank, so 48 ÷ 4 × 2 = 24, not 6.

Missing digits

Divisible by 5 and 8: the last digit is 0, and the last three digits must be divisible by 8.
Divisible by 9 and 11: apply both rules and solve for the digits.

Common traps

HCF × LCM equals the product only for two numbers.

If a number leaves remainder r on division by d, write it as d×q + r, with r smaller than d.

√0.09 = 0.3, not 0.03.

Divisible by 8 does not mean divisible by 3, so it need not be divisible by 24.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
The HCF of 36 and 48 is 12. Their LCM is

  1. 144
  2. 156
  3. 172
  4. 192

Solution

  1. 36 × 48 = 1728.
  2. LCM = product / HCF = 1728 / 12 = 144.

Answer: A. 144 is a multiple of both 36 and 48, and the identity gives no smaller common multiple.

Example 2
Which number is divisible by 11?

  1. 2345
  2. 3729
  3. 4505
  4. 6914

Solution

  1. Alternating sum of 3729 is 3 − 7 + 2 − 9 = −11.
  2. −11 is a multiple of 11, so 3729 is divisible by 11.
  3. The other alternating sums are −2, −6 and −6.

Answer: B. 3729 ÷ 11 = 339, an exact division.

Example 3
The least positive number which leaves remainder 3 when divided by 4, 6 and 8 is

  1. 21
  2. 24
  3. 27
  4. 48

Solution

  1. Such a number is LCM(4, 6, 8) × k + 3.
  2. LCM of 4, 6 and 8 is 24.
  3. The least positive value is 24 + 3 = 27.

Answer: C. 27 ÷ 4, 27 ÷ 6 and 27 ÷ 8 each leave remainder 3.

Example 4
The value of √0.0169 is

  1. 0.013
  2. 0.13
  3. 1.3
  4. 0.0013

Solution

  1. 0.0169 = 169/10000.
  2. √169 = 13 and √10000 = 100.
  3. 13/100 = 0.13.

Answer: B. The decimal pairs under the root are 01 and 69.

Example 5
The HCF of 2/3, 4/5 and 6/7 is

  1. 2/105
  2. 2/3
  3. 4/105
  4. 12/35

Solution

  1. HCF of the numerators 2, 4 and 6 is 2.
  2. LCM of the denominators 3, 5 and 7 is 105.
  3. HCF of the fractions is 2/105.

Answer: A. The LCM of fractions uses the other arrangement: LCM of numerators over HCF of denominators.

Example 6
(CGL pattern) If 36 ÷ 4 + 3 × A − 30 ÷ 5 + 2 = 14, then A is

  1. −3
  2. 3
  3. 2
  4. 4

Solution

  1. Do ÷ and × first: 9 + 3A − 6 + 2 = 14.
  2. 3A + 5 = 14, so A = 3.

Answer: B. Adding before dividing is the trap that gives a wrong A.

Example 7
(CGL pattern) The 7-digit number 53842*# is divisible by both 8 and 5. The digits * and # are

  1. 4 and 0
  2. 5 and 0
  3. 3 and 0
  4. 0 and 5

Solution

  1. Divisible by 5 and even (because of 8), so # = 0.
  2. The last three digits 2*0 must be divisible by 8.
  3. 240 ÷ 8 = 30. 250 and 230 are not multiples of 8.

Answer: A. # = 5 would make the number odd, so it cannot be divisible by 8.

Example 8
(CGL pattern) The average of the squares of the first 30 natural numbers is

  1. 310.17
  2. 315.17
  3. 320.17
  4. 305.17

Solution

  1. Sum of squares = n(n+1)(2n+1)/6, so the average = (n+1)(2n+1)/6.
  2. 31 × 61 / 6 = 1891/6 = 315.17.

Answer: B. Dividing the sum formula by n gives the shortcut directly.

Example 9
(CGL pattern) Three runners complete a circular track in 12, 18 and 24 minutes. They start together at 6:00 a.m. They are next together at the start at

  1. 6:36 a.m.
  2. 7:12 a.m.
  3. 7:24 a.m.
  4. 8:00 a.m.

Solution

  1. They meet at the start after LCM(12, 18, 24) minutes.
  2. LCM = 72 minutes = 1 hour 12 minutes.

Answer: B. HCF would be the wrong tool. Meeting again is a common multiple.

Practice yourself

Practice 1
The LCM of 12 and 18 is

  1. 36
  2. 48
  3. 54
  4. 72
Show answer

Answer: A. 12 = 2²×3 and 18 = 2×3², so the LCM is 2²×3² = 36.

Practice 2
√1.44 equals

  1. 0.12
  2. 1.2
  3. 12
  4. 0.012
Show answer

Answer: B. 1.44 = 144/100, and the square root is 12/10 = 1.2.

Practice 3
The greatest number that divides 43, 91 and 183 and leaves the same remainder each time is

  1. 4
  2. 8
  3. 13
  4. 16
Show answer

Answer: A. Same remainder means the divisor divides the differences: 91 − 43 = 48, 183 − 91 = 92, 183 − 43 = 140. HCF of 48, 92 and 140 is 4. Check: 43, 91 and 183 each leave remainder 3 on division by 4. 8 fails because 183 leaves 7.

Practice 4
Which is the smallest fraction?

  1. 3/5
  2. 5/8
  3. 7/12
  4. 2/3
Show answer

Answer: C. Cross-check: 7/12 = 0.583, while 3/5 = 0.6, 5/8 = 0.625 and 2/3 ≈ 0.667.

Practice 5
The unit digit of 7²³ is

  1. 1
  2. 3
  3. 7
  4. 9
Show answer

Answer: B. The cycle is 7, 9, 3, 1. 23 ÷ 4 leaves 3, so take the 3rd term: 3.

Practice 6
The number of factors of 72 is

  1. 8
  2. 10
  3. 12
  4. 14
Show answer

Answer: C. 72 = 2³ × 3². Factors = (3+1)(2+1) = 12.

Practice 7
1 + 2 + 3 + … + 50 equals

  1. 1225
  2. 1250
  3. 1275
  4. 2550
Show answer

Answer: C. 50 × 51 / 2 = 1275.

Practice 8
The remainder when 25 × 37 is divided by 6 is

  1. 0
  2. 1
  3. 3
  4. 5
Show answer

Answer: B. 25 leaves 1 and 37 leaves 1. 1 × 1 = 1. Check: 925 = 6 × 154 + 1.

Practice 9
1² + 2² + … + 10² equals

  1. 285
  2. 355
  3. 385
  4. 505
Show answer

Answer: C. 10 × 11 × 21 / 6 = 385.

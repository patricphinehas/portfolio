Quantitative Aptitude · Tier-I and Paper-I

Partnership

A partnership question splits a profit in proportion to the money each person left in the business, and for how long. If everyone invests for the same time, time cancels and you use only the capitals.

When someone joins late or withdraws money, multiply each capital by its own number of months.

What SSC lists

  • Partnership business and division of profit.
  • Capitals invested for the same time.
  • Capitals invested for different periods.
  • A change of capital during the year.

Rules and formulas

Same time

Profit share = capital share.
If capitals are in the ratio a:b, profit is split in a:b.

Different time

Use capital × time for each partner.
Months are easier than fractions of a year. Use the same unit for everyone.

A partner joins late

If B joins after 4 months of a 12-month year, B’s time is 8 months, not 4.

Working partner

If the question gives one partner a salary, subtract that salary from the profit before you split the rest. Do this only when the question says so.

Quick methods

Profit ratio = C₁T₁ : C₂T₂ : C₃T₃.
Capitals from profits and times: C₁ : C₂ = (P₁/T₁) : (P₂/T₂).
Times from profits and capitals: T₁ : T₂ = (P₁/C₁) : (P₂/C₂).
If capital changes mid-year, add the capital-months of each block.

Common traps

Do not split profit in the ratio of capitals when the times are different.

“After 4 months” means the remaining time is 8 months in a one-year business.

A ratio 2:3:5 has 10 parts, not 5.

The profit in the question is the amount to be shared. Do not add the capitals into that profit.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
A invests Rs 20000 and B invests Rs 30000 for the same time. Out of a profit of Rs 5000, A’s share is

  1. Rs 1500
  2. Rs 2000
  3. Rs 2500
  4. Rs 3000

Solution

  1. Ratio of capitals = 2:3.
  2. A’s fraction is 2/5.
  3. 2/5 of 5000 = 2000.

Answer: B. B’s share is the remaining 3000.

Example 2
A invests Rs 10000 for 12 months and B invests Rs 15000 for 8 months. The profit ratio A:B is

  1. 1:1
  2. 2:3
  3. 4:5
  4. 3:4

Solution

  1. A’s capital-months = 10000 × 12 = 120000.
  2. B’s capital-months = 15000 × 8 = 120000.
  3. The ratio is 1:1.

Answer: A. The larger capital was invested for fewer months, and the products match.

Example 3
A, B and C invest in the ratio 2:3:5. The total profit is Rs 20000. C’s share is

  1. Rs 4000
  2. Rs 6000
  3. Rs 8000
  4. Rs 10000

Solution

  1. Total parts = 10.
  2. C has 5 parts, which is half the profit.
  3. C’s share = 10000.

Answer: D. A gets 4000 and B gets 6000.

Example 4
A starts a business with Rs 40000. After 4 months B joins with Rs 60000. The profit ratio A:B at the end of the year is

  1. 1:1
  2. 2:3
  3. 4:3
  4. 3:2

Solution

  1. A’s time is 12 months: 40000 × 12 = 480000.
  2. B’s time is 8 months: 60000 × 8 = 480000.
  3. Ratio = 1:1.

Answer: A. B joined after 4 months, so B was in the business for 8 months.

Example 5
A and B invest in the ratio 3:2. After 6 months A withdraws half of A’s capital. The year-end profit is Rs 51000. A’s share is

  1. Rs 24000
  2. Rs 27000
  3. Rs 30000
  4. Rs 34000

Solution

  1. Take capitals as 3x and 2x.
  2. A’s capital-months = 3x×6 + 1.5x×6 = 27x. B’s = 2x×12 = 24x.
  3. Ratio = 27:24 = 9:8. A’s share = 9/17 × 51000 = 27000.

Answer: B. 51000 ÷ 17 = 3000, and 9 parts are 27000.

  1. Rs 1000
  2. Rs 1200
  3. Rs 1400
  4. Rs 1600
Show answer

Answer: C. B’s fraction is 7/12 of 2400 = 1400.

Practice 2
A invests for 6 months and B for 12 months. Their capitals are equal. Profit is shared in the ratio

  1. 1:1
  2. 1:2
  3. 2:1
  4. 6:12 only after adding capitals
Show answer

Answer: B. Equal capitals mean the ratio equals the times, 6:12 = 1:2.

Practice 3
Three partners share profit in the ratio 1:2:3. If the total profit is Rs 12000, the smallest share is

  1. Rs 1000
  2. Rs 2000
  3. Rs 3000
  4. Rs 4000
Show answer

Answer: B. Total parts are 6. One part is 2000.

Practice 4
A starts with Rs 20000. B joins 3 months later with the same capital. In a 12-month year, A’s capital-months divided by B’s capital-months equal

  1. 4/3
  2. 3/4
  3. 1
  4. 2
Show answer

Answer: A. A is invested for 12 months and B for 9. The ratio of capital-months is 12/9 = 4/3.

Practice 5
A, B and C invest Rs 2000, 3000 and 5000 for 12, 8 and 6 months. The profit ratio is

  1. 2:3:5
  2. 4:4:5
  3. 1:1:1
  4. 4:5:4
Show answer

Answer: B. 24000 : 24000 : 30000 = 4:4:5.

Practice 6
A and B share profit 2:3 and were invested for 4 and 6 months. Their capitals were in the ratio

  1. 1:1
  2. 2:3
  3. 4:9
  4. 3:2
Show answer

Answer: A. (2/4) : (3/6) = 1/2 : 1/2 = 1:1.

Practice 7
A starts with Rs 6000 and adds Rs 2000 after 6 months. B invests Rs 10000 for the year. Out of Rs 17000 profit, A gets

  1. Rs 6000
  2. Rs 8500
  3. Rs 7000
  4. Rs 10000
Show answer

Answer: C. A = 6000×6 + 8000×6 = 84000. B = 120000. Ratio 7:10, so A gets 7/17 × 17000 = 7000.

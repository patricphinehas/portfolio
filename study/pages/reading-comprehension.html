English · Tier-I and Tier-II English

Reading comprehension

A comprehension passage asks what the writer actually said. The answer is in the passage, or it is a short step from a sentence that is in the passage. Your outside opinion does not score.

The Tier-II note says you may see more than one paragraph: one simple narrative, and others closer to a report or an editorial. Read the question, then look for the sentence that can support it. This passage is original practice text, not a past paper.

What SSC lists

  • Comprehension passages and questions based on them.
  • Main idea, a stated detail, a word in context, and a title.
  • The instruction in the notice: paragraphs may include a simple story and passages on current-style reports.
  • Vocabulary in context, which is a reading skill as much as a word list.

Rules and formulas

Order of work

Read the questions once. Then read the passage. Then answer, with a finger on the sentence that supports you.

Detail

A detail question uses names and numbers from the passage. If the number is not there, do not import one.

Main idea

The main idea covers the whole passage, not one example in the middle.

Word in context

A word’s meaning is the meaning it has in that sentence. A dictionary sense that does not fit the sentence is wrong.

Common traps

An option that is true in real life, but absent from the passage, is wrong.

An option that repeats a word from the passage and changes the claim is wrong.

“The author implies” still needs a sentence to lean on. It is not a guess.

Do not spend the English section’s 40 minutes in Tier-II on one passage. Mark it and move if two readings have not produced an answer.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
Passage: Meera had 40 minutes for English. She did the short grammar items first and left one long passage for the end. She finished the grammar with 16 minutes left, and she attempted every question in the passage. What did she do first?

  1. The long passage
  2. The short grammar items
  3. A review of old mocks
  4. Nothing, because time was short

Solution

  1. The second sentence says she did the short grammar items first.
  2. The passage does not mention a review of mocks.

Answer: B. The 16 minutes are about what was left, not about what she did first.

Example 2
In the same passage, how much time was left when the grammar was finished?

  1. 40 minutes
  2. 24 minutes
  3. 16 minutes
  4. The passage does not say

Solution

  1. The sentence gives 16 minutes left.
  2. 40 minutes is the whole English block she had.

Answer: C. Do not subtract again. The passage already states the remainder.

Example 3
In the same passage, which statement is supported?

  1. Meera left the passage unattempted
  2. Meera attempted every question in the passage
  3. Meera scored full marks
  4. Meera skipped grammar

Solution

  1. The last clause says she attempted every question in the passage.
  2. It does not say she scored full marks.

Answer: B. Attempted is not the same as correct.

Example 4
Passage: The town well dried up in May. Tankers arrived twice a week. Residents stored water in drums, and the school shortened its day. A fair title is

  1. A new dam is inaugurated
  2. How a town coped with a dry well
  3. Rainfall records of the state
  4. The school’s annual prize day

Solution

  1. Every sentence is about the dry well and the response.
  2. The title has to cover the well, the tankers and the school, not one of them only.

Answer: B. No dam, no rainfall table and no prize day is in the passage.

Practice yourself

Attempt these without opening the answer. Then check the toggle.

Practice 1
From the well passage: the tankers came

  1. every day
  2. twice a week
  3. only in June
  4. never
Show answer

Answer: B. The passage says twice a week.

Practice 2
From the well passage, the school

  1. closed for the year
  2. shortened its day
  3. moved to another town
  4. built the well
Show answer

Answer: B. The last sentence says the school shortened its day.

Practice 3
“Residents stored water in drums” supports which idea?

  1. Water was being kept for later use
  2. The well was full
  3. Tankers were cancelled
  4. Drums were banned
Show answer

Answer: A. Storing means keeping water, which fits a shortage.

Practice 4
Which option is not supported by the well passage?

  1. The well dried up in May
  2. Tankers came
  3. A new pipeline was opened in June
  4. The school changed its hours
Show answer

Answer: C. A pipeline is not mentioned. The other three are.

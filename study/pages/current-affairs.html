Current affairs: how to revise

Current affairs is the part of General Awareness that a static page cannot freeze. Appointments, sports results, schemes and prizes move. This page teaches the net you should cast, and it uses a few stable international facts that often sit beside the news. It does not pretend to be this month’s capsule.

From October 2026, build a rolling file of the last six to twelve months. The Commission’s own syllabus asks for current events and for everyday science. It does not publish a fixed list of schemes.

What SSC lists

  • Current events, as required in both tiers.
  • A method: what to collect, and what to ignore.
  • Stable international headquarters that are often revised next to the news.
  • A warning: office-holders on this page would be stale. Look them up.

Rules and formulas

What to collect

For each month, note: major government schemes and their ministries, appointments to constitutional posts, sports hosts and winners, awards, summits, and one science or space item.
Prefer Press Information Bureau releases and a newspaper over a random social-media list.

How to store it

One line per fact: what, who, where, when. If you cannot say which ministry a scheme belongs to, the fact is not finished.
Revise monthly, and again in the last two weeks. Old capsules from a previous exam year are a source of wrong answers.

Stable neighbours to the news

United Nations headquarters: New York. World Health Organization: Geneva.
The International Monetary Fund and the World Bank: Washington, DC.
SAARC secretariat: Kathmandu.
These are slow-changing facts. Confirm them if a question is about a body that has moved, which is rare.

What not to do

Do not memorise a cutoff, a rank list, or a “guaranteed 20 questions” claim.
Do not treat a coaching PDF from a previous year as current without checking the date on the fact.

Common traps

A scheme’s name is useless without the ministry and the year you saw it announced.

The headquarters above are static GK. The name of the current Secretary-General, or the current WHO Director-General, is not. Check it.

Sports questions love the host city and the winner, not the full scorecard.

If two sources disagree about a date, prefer the official release.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
Which habit produces a usable current-affairs note?

  1. Copying an undated list of 500 facts
  2. Writing what, who, where and when for each fact from a dated source
  3. Memorising last year’s prize list only
  4. Skipping schemes because they feel long

Solution

  1. A fact you can place in time can be revised.
  2. An undated pile cannot be corrected when the office-holder changes.

Answer: B. The syllabus asks for current events, so the date is part of the answer.

Example 2
The headquarters of the World Health Organization is in

  1. New York
  2. Geneva
  3. Paris
  4. Kathmandu

Solution

  1. WHO is based in Geneva.
  2. The UN headquarters is in New York. SAARC’s secretariat is in Kathmandu.

Answer: B. Paris is a common guess because of UNESCO. UNESCO is in Paris. WHO is in Geneva.

Example 3
The SAARC secretariat is in

  1. Dhaka
  2. Kathmandu
  3. Colombo
  4. New Delhi

Solution

  1. Kathmandu is the seat of the secretariat.
  2. Member countries include neighbours of India, but the secretariat is not in Delhi.

Answer: B. Do not confuse a member capital with the secretariat.

Example 4
Before the exam, the list of new schemes should be checked against

  1. an undated blog
  2. a Press Information Bureau release or the ministry’s own page
  3. a cutoff predictor
  4. last decade’s guidebook only

Solution

  1. Schemes are government facts.
  2. The official release is the source that matches the fact.

Answer: B. A guidebook can explain the background. It cannot be the only source for a scheme launched after it was printed.

Practice 1
The United Nations headquarters is in

  1. Geneva
  2. New York
  3. Vienna
  4. The Hague
Show answer

Answer: B. New York. Several UN agencies sit in Geneva, which is the trap.

Practice 2
The World Bank is headquartered in

  1. Washington, DC
  2. London
  3. Manila
  4. Beijing
Show answer

Answer: A. Washington, DC, as is the IMF.

Practice 3
A sports current-affairs note should normally record

  1. every player’s biography
  2. the event, the winner and the host or venue
  3. only the ticket price
  4. the coach’s school
Show answer

Answer: B. Event, winner, place. Add a record if the report says one was broken.

Practice 4
Which fact should be rechecked in the month of the exam?

  1. The author of Jana Gana Mana
  2. The current chairperson of a constitutional body
  3. The year of the Battle of Plassey
  4. The formula of common salt
Show answer

Answer: B. Office-holders change. Tagore, 1757 and NaCl do not.

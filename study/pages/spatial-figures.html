Reasoning · Tier-I and Paper-I

Figures, folding and cubes

Figural questions ask you to track a shape: a paper folded and punched, a figure hidden inside a larger one, or a cube painted and cut. The notice names space visualisation, punched hole and pattern folding, embedded figures, and figural pattern completion.

Without a printed figure, train the counts that these questions use. A painted cube is the cleanest of them, and it returns often.

What SSC lists

  • Space visualisation and figural classification habits.
  • Punched hole, pattern folding and unfolding.
  • Embedded figures: a small shape sitting inside a larger drawing.
  • A cube painted on some faces and cut into smaller cubes.

Rules and formulas

Folding

Each fold stacks layers. A hole punched through the stack appears in every layer when the paper is opened.
Unfold in reverse order.

Embedded figure

The small shape must appear with the same sides, the same angles and the same orientation. Recent papers say “rotation is not allowed”, so a turned or reflected copy does not count unless the question says it does.

Painted cube

A cube cut into n × n × n smaller cubes has n³ pieces.
Three faces painted: the 8 corners, if all six faces of the big cube were painted.
Two faces painted: 12 edges × (n − 2) pieces, for n at least 2.
One face painted: 6 faces × (n − 2)².
Unpainted inside: (n − 2)³.

Counting

Count a region once. Mark each triangle as you count it, or you will count the same one under two descriptions.

Dice

A standard die has opposite faces adding to 7: 1–6, 2–5, 3–4.
A face seen next to four different faces across the views is opposite the one face never seen with it.
Two views sharing one face: read clockwise from that face in both views; faces in matching positions are opposite.

Mirror and water images

Clock in a mirror: subtract the time from 11:60. 3:20 becomes 8:40.
A vertical mirror swaps left and right. A water image swaps top and bottom.

Counting figures

Triangle with lines from the apex: if the base is cut into n parts, there are n(n+1)/2 triangles. Each extra line drawn right across the triangle adds another full set: with one cross line, double it.
Squares in an n × n grid: 1² + 2² + … + n². A 3 × 3 grid has 14.
A square with both diagonals has 8 triangles: 4 small ones and 4 halves.
Count by size, smallest first, and tick each one off.

Paper folding and punching

Unfold in reverse order. Each unfolding reflects every hole across that fold line.
One fold → 2 holes. Two folds → 4 holes. Three folds → 8 holes, if the punch goes through every layer.
The new hole sits at the same distance from the fold line, on the other side.

Embedded figures

The given shape must appear with the same size, shape and orientation unless the question allows rotation.
A mirrored copy is a different figure.

Common traps

For a 3×3×3 cube with all faces painted, the very centre cube has no paint. There is one such cube, not zero.

Corner cubes have three painted faces, not two. Two painted faces sit on the edges, away from the corners.

A hole punched through two layers makes two holes when opened, in mirror positions across the fold.

An embedded figure that is stretched is not a match.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
A cube is painted on all six faces and cut into 27 smaller equal cubes. How many smaller cubes have exactly three faces painted?

  1. 4
  2. 6
  3. 8
  4. 12

Solution

  1. The big cube is 3×3×3.
  2. Only a corner meets three faces.
  3. A cube has 8 corners.

Answer: C. This count does not depend on the cut, as long as every corner survives as its own small cube, which it does for n = 3.

Example 2
In that same 3×3×3 painted cube, how many smaller cubes have no face painted?

  1. 0
  2. 1
  3. 2
  4. 6

Solution

  1. The unpainted cubes are the inner cube of side 3−2 = 1.
  2. (n−2)³ = 1.

Answer: B. It is the single cube in the very centre.

Example 3
In that same cube, how many smaller cubes have exactly one face painted?

  1. 4
  2. 6
  3. 8
  4. 12

Solution

  1. Each of the 6 faces has a centre square that is not on an edge.
  2. (n−2)² = 1 per face.
  3. 6 × 1 = 6.

Answer: B. The edge cubes have two faces painted, so they are not in this count.

Example 4
A square paper is folded in half once, and one hole is punched through the folded paper. When the paper is opened, the number of holes is

  1. 1
  2. 2
  3. 4
  4. 8

Solution

  1. The fold makes two layers.
  2. The punch goes through both.
  3. Opening the paper shows 2 holes.

Answer: B. A second fold, before the punch, would stack four layers and give four holes.

Example 5
(CGL pattern) A die with faces 1 to 6 shows 4, 2 and 6 in one view and 4, 1 and 5 in another. The face opposite 4 is

  1. 1
  2. 3
  3. 2
  4. 6

Solution

  1. 4 is seen next to 2, 6, 1 and 5.
  2. The only face never beside 4 is 3.

Answer: B. Adjacent faces can never be opposite.

Example 6
(CGL pattern) A clock shows 4:25. Its mirror image shows

  1. 8:35
  2. 7:25
  3. 7:35
  4. 8:25

Solution

  1. Subtract from 11:60.
  2. 11:60 − 4:25 = 7:35.

Answer: C. Using 12:00 instead of 11:60 handles the minutes wrongly.

Example 7
(CGL pattern) How many triangles are there in the given figure?

  1. 6
  2. 8
  3. 10
  4. 12

Solution

  1. The base is cut into 4 parts by the 3 inner lines.
  2. Triangles = 4 × 5 / 2 = 10.
  3. By size: 4 single, 3 double, 2 triple, 1 whole.

Answer: C. Counting only the 4 small ones is the usual miss.

Example 8
(CGL pattern) How many squares are there in the given figure?

  1. 9
  2. 10
  3. 13
  4. 14

Solution

  1. Small 1 × 1 squares: 9.
  2. 2 × 2 squares: 4.
  3. The whole 3 × 3 square: 1. Total 14.

Answer: D. 9 counts only the smallest squares.

Example 9
(CGL pattern) A square paper is folded along the dashed line as shown, and a hole is punched. How does it look when unfolded?

Solution

  1. The right half was folded onto the left half along the vertical centre line.
  2. Unfolding reflects the hole across that line: (20, 25) gives a second hole at (80, 25).
  3. Both holes stay at the same height.

Answer: A. Option B reflects across a horizontal line, which was never a fold.

Example 10
(CGL pattern) Which option is the mirror image of the figure when the mirror is placed along MN?

Solution

  1. MN is a vertical line, so left and right swap.
  2. Top stays top and bottom stays bottom.
  3. The bars that pointed right now point left; the dot moves to the left side.

Answer: C. Option A is the water image (top and bottom swapped). Option B is a half-turn.

  3. 36
  4. 48

Answer: B. Each of 12 edges contributes (4−2) = 2 cubes. 12×2 = 24.

A paper folded in half twice, then punched once through all layers, shows how many holes when fully opened?

  1. 2
  2. 3
  3. 4
  4. 8

Answer: C. Two folds make four layers, so one punch makes four holes.

  1. 27

  4. 8

  1. 24
  2. 48

  2. 15
  3. 20
  4. 24

  1. 16
  2. 25
  3. 30
  4. 36

Practice 10
(CGL pattern) How many triangles does a square with both diagonals contain?

  1. 4
  2. 6
  3. 8
  4. 10
Show answer

Answer: C. 4 small triangles at the centre, plus 4 halves cut by each diagonal (2 per diagonal).

(CGL pattern) A square paper is folded twice as shown and punched once. How does it look when unfolded?

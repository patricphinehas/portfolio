Quantitative Aptitude · Tier-I and Paper-I

Profit, loss and discount

Profit and loss compare the selling price with the cost price. Discount compares the selling price with the marked price. A shopkeeper question often uses both: the tag is marked up, then a discount is offered, and you are asked for the real profit.

Always name the base before you write a percent.

What SSC lists

  • Profit and loss percent on cost price.
  • Finding cost price from selling price, and the reverse.
  • Marked price, discount, and successive discounts.
  • A markup followed by a discount.

Rules and formulas

Profit and loss

Profit = SP − CP. Loss = CP − SP.
Profit % = (Profit / CP) × 100. Loss % = (Loss / CP) × 100.
SP = CP × (100 ± profit or loss percent) / 100.

Discount

Discount = MP − SP.
Discount % = (Discount / MP) × 100.
SP = MP × (100 − discount %) / 100.

Successive discounts

Discounts of a% and b% are equivalent to a single discount of a + b − (a×b)/100 percent.

Equal sale, opposite percent

If two articles are sold at the same price, one at r% profit and one at r% loss, the net result is a loss of (r²/100) % on the combined cost.

Quick methods

If the CP of x articles = the SP of y articles, profit % = (x − y)/y × 100. Negative means loss.
False weight: giving w grams for 1000 grams at cost price gives gain = (1000 − w)/w × 100 %.
To make r% profit after d% discount: MP = CP × (100 + r)/(100 − d).
CP from SP: CP = SP × 100/(100 + profit%) or SP × 100/(100 − loss%).

Common traps

Profit percent uses cost price as the base, not the selling price, unless the question says so.

Discount percent uses marked price as the base.

Two successive discounts of 20% and 10% are not a 30% discount.

Selling two things at the same price, with 10% profit and 10% loss, is not a zero result. There is a small loss.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
An article costs Rs 400 and is sold for Rs 460. The profit percent is

  1. 12%
  2. 15%
  3. 16%
  4. 20%

Solution

  1. Profit = 60.
  2. Profit % = (60/400) × 100 = 15.

Answer: B. The base is the cost, 400.

Example 2
By selling an article for Rs 540 a trader loses 10%. The cost price is

  1. Rs 486
  2. Rs 594
  3. Rs 600
  4. Rs 640

Solution

  1. SP is 90% of CP.
  2. CP = 540 × 100/90 = 600.

Answer: C. A 10% loss on 600 is 60, and 600 − 60 = 540.

Example 3
Successive discounts of 20% and 10% are equal to a single discount of

  1. 28%
  2. 30%
  3. 32%
  4. 25%

Solution

  1. Equivalent discount = 20 + 10 − (20×10)/100.
  2. That is 30 − 2 = 28%.

Answer: A. On a marked price of 100 the selling price is 80, then 72. The discount is 28.

Example 4
A trader marks goods 40% above cost and allows a discount of 10%. The profit percent is

  1. 26%
  2. 30%
  3. 36%
  4. 50%

Solution

  1. Take CP as 100. Marked price = 140.
  2. SP = 90% of 140 = 126.
  3. Profit = 26 on 100, so 26%.

Answer: A. The discount is on the marked price, not on the cost.

Example 5
Two articles are sold for Rs 990 each. On one the gain is 10% and on the other the loss is 10%. The net result is

  1. no gain, no loss
  2. 1% gain
  3. 1% loss
  4. 10% loss

Solution

  1. CP of the first = 990 × 100/110 = 900.
  2. CP of the second = 990 × 100/90 = 1100.
  3. Total CP = 2000 and total SP = 1980, so the loss is 20, which is 1%.

Answer: C. The shortcut for this pattern is a loss of (10²/100)% = 1%.

Example 6
(CGL pattern) An article marked ₹2,000 is sold after successive discounts of 10%, 20% and 25%. The price paid is

  1. ₹1,000
  2. ₹1,080
  3. ₹1,100
  4. ₹900

Solution

  1. Multiply by each remaining fraction.
  2. 2000 × 0.9 × 0.8 × 0.75 = 1080.

Answer: B. Adding the discounts to 55% would give ₹900, the trap.

Practice yourself

Attempt these without opening the answer. Then check the toggle.

Practice 1
CP is Rs 250 and profit is 20%. The selling price is

  1. Rs 270
  2. Rs 280
  3. Rs 300
  4. Rs 320
Show answer

Answer: C. SP = 250 × 1.2 = 300.

Practice 2
Marked price is Rs 800 and the discount is 15%. The selling price is

  1. Rs 640
  2. Rs 680
  3. Rs 720
  4. Rs 750
Show answer

Answer: B. 15% of 800 is 120, so SP is 680.

Practice 3
A 25% profit is made by selling a pen for Rs 75. The cost price is

  1. Rs 50
  2. Rs 56
  3. Rs 60
  4. Rs 65
Show answer

Answer: C. 75 is 125% of CP, so CP = 75 × 100/125 = 60.

Practice 4
Successive discounts of 10% and 20% on Rs 250 give a selling price of

  1. Rs 175
  2. Rs 180
  3. Rs 185
  4. Rs 200
Show answer

Answer: B. Equivalent discount is 28%. 72% of 250 is 180.

Practice 5
The cost price of 20 articles equals the selling price of 16. The profit percent is

  1. 20%
  2. 25%
  3. 16%
  4. 30%
Show answer

Answer: B. (20 − 16)/16 × 100 = 25%.

Practice 6
A dealer gives 900 g instead of 1 kg and sells at cost price. His gain is

  1. 10%
  2. 11 1/9%
  3. 9 1/11%
  4. 12.5%
Show answer

Answer: B. 100/900 × 100 = 11 1/9%.

Practice 7
To earn 20% profit after a 20% discount, goods must be marked above cost by

  1. 40%
  2. 44%
  3. 50%
  4. 60%
Show answer

Answer: C. MP/CP = 120/80 = 1.5, so 50% above cost.

Practice 8
Selling at Rs 720 gives a loss of 20%. The cost price is

  1. Rs 900
  2. Rs 864
  3. Rs 880
  4. Rs 960
Show answer

Answer: A. 720 × 100/80 = 900.

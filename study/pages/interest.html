Simple and compound interest

Interest is the rent paid for using money. Simple interest grows by the same amount every year. Compound interest adds this year’s interest into the principal, so the next year’s interest is larger.

For two years, learn the small gap between compound and simple interest. It is a standard two-line question.

What SSC lists

  • Simple interest and the amount.
  • Compound interest, usually for 2 years, sometimes with half-yearly compounding.
  • The difference between compound and simple interest for 2 years.
  • Finding rate or time when the amount is given.

Rules and formulas

Simple interest

SI = (P × R × T) / 100.
Amount = P + SI.
Here T is in years and R is the percent per year.

Compound interest

Amount after n years = P × (1 + R/100)^n.
CI = Amount − P.
Half-yearly: use R/2 as the rate and 2n as the number of periods.

Two-year gap

For 2 years, CI − SI = P × (R/100)².
For 3 years, CI − SI = P × R² × (300 + R) / 1,000,000.

Doubling at simple interest

If a sum doubles in T years at simple interest, R = 100/T.
The interest earned equals the principal.

Quick methods

Effective rate for 2 years at r% compound = 2r + r²/100 percent. At 10% it is 21%. At 20% it is 44%.
A sum becomes n times in T years at SI: R = 100(n − 1)/T.
At CI, if a sum doubles in T years, it becomes 4 times in 2T years and 8 times in 3T years.
CI − SI for 2 years = (SI of one year) × R/100.

Common traps

Compound interest is not P × R × T / 100. That is simple interest.

Half-yearly compounding changes both the rate and the number of periods.

The two-year difference formula is not for three years.

“Amount” includes the principal. “Interest” does not.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
The simple interest on Rs 8000 at 5% per annum for 3 years is

  1. Rs 1000
  2. Rs 1200
  3. Rs 1500
  4. Rs 1600

Solution

  1. SI = (8000 × 5 × 3) / 100.
  2. 8000/100 = 80, and 80 × 15 = 1200.

Answer: B. The amount would be 8000 + 1200 = 9200. The question asks only for interest.

Example 2
The compound interest on Rs 10000 at 10% per annum for 2 years is

  1. Rs 2000
  2. Rs 2100
  3. Rs 2200
  4. Rs 12100

Solution

  1. Amount = 10000 × (1.1)² = 10000 × 1.21 = 12100.
  2. CI = 12100 − 10000 = 2100.

Answer: B. 12100 is the amount, not the interest.

Example 3
The difference between compound and simple interest on Rs 5000 at 10% per annum for 2 years is

  1. Rs 40
  2. Rs 50
  3. Rs 100
  4. Rs 500

Solution

  1. Difference = P × (R/100)².
  2. 5000 × (0.1)² = 5000 × 0.01 = 50.

Answer: B. SI is 1000 and CI is 1050.

Example 4
A sum of money doubles itself in 5 years at simple interest. The rate is

  1. 10% per annum
  2. 15% per annum
  3. 20% per annum
  4. 25% per annum

Solution

  1. Interest earned in 5 years equals the principal.
  2. P = (P × R × 5) / 100.
  3. R = 20.

Answer: C. In 5 years the interest is 100% of the principal, so the yearly rate is 20%.

Example 5
In how many years will Rs 4000 amount to Rs 5200 at 10% simple interest?

  1. 2
  2. 3
  3. 4
  4. 5

Solution

  1. Interest needed = 5200 − 4000 = 1200.
  2. 1200 = (4000 × 10 × T) / 100 = 400T.
  3. T = 3.

Answer: B. Each year the interest is Rs 400, and 1200 needs 3 years.

Example 6
(CGL pattern) The simple interest on a sum is 9/25 of the sum, and the rate percent per annum equals the number of years. The rate is

  1. 4%
  2. 5%
  3. 6%
  4. 9%

Solution

  1. SI/P = R × T / 100 = 9/25.
  2. With R = T: R² / 100 = 9/25, so R² = 36.
  3. R = 6%.

Answer: C. The time is also 6 years.

Practice 2
Rs 8000 is lent at 10% compounded half-yearly for 1 year. The compound interest is

  1. Rs 800
  2. Rs 820
  3. Rs 840
  4. Rs 880
Show answer

Answer: B. Two periods at 5%. Amount = 8000 × 1.05 × 1.05 = 8820. Interest = 820.

Practice 3
The simple interest on a sum for 4 years at 5% is Rs 400. The principal is

  1. Rs 1600
  2. Rs 2000
  3. Rs 2400
  4. Rs 4000
Show answer

Answer: B. 400 = (P × 5 × 4) / 100, so P = 2000.

Practice 4
At compound interest of 10%, Rs 2000 becomes how much after 2 years?

  1. Rs 2200
  2. Rs 2400
  3. Rs 2420
  4. Rs 2600
Show answer

Answer: C. 2000 × 1.21 = 2420.

Practice 5
The effective rate of compound interest for 2 years at 20% per annum is

  1. 40%
  2. 42%
  3. 44%
  4. 48%
Show answer

Answer: C. 2 × 20 + 20²/100 = 44%.

Practice 6
A sum becomes three times itself in 10 years at simple interest. The rate is

  1. 20%
  2. 10%
  3. 15%
  4. 30%
Show answer

Answer: A. R = 100 × (3 − 1)/10 = 20%.

Practice 7
A sum doubles in 4 years at compound interest. It becomes 8 times in

  1. 8 years
  2. 12 years
  3. 16 years
  4. 24 years
Show answer

Answer: B. 8 = 2³, so it takes 3 × 4 = 12 years.

Practice 8
The compound interest on Rs 1000 at 20% for 2 years is

  1. Rs 400
  2. Rs 420
  3. Rs 480
  4. Rs 440
Show answer

Answer: D. Effective rate 44%, so 44% of 1000 = 440.

Practice 9
The simple interest for one year at 10% is Rs 200. The difference between CI and SI for 2 years is

  1. Rs 10
  2. Rs 20
  3. Rs 40
  4. Rs 200
Show answer

Answer: B. 200 × 10/100 = 20.

Reasoning · Tier-I and Paper-I

Ranking, order and arithmetical reasoning

Problem solving in the notice covers a short puzzle you can finish with one diagram: a rank in a row, an order of ages, or a small family of equations. The arithmetic is easy. The work is keeping the position count honest.

When a person is counted from both ends, that person was counted twice, so you subtract 1.

What SSC lists

  • Problem solving and analysis.
  • Ranking from the left and from the right.
  • Order of age or height.
  • Arithmetical reasoning such as ages, which uses ordinary algebra.

Rules and formulas

Both ends

If a person is p-th from the left and q-th from the right in a single row, the number of people is p + q − 1.

Between

If A is a-th from the left and B is b-th from the left, with b greater than a, the people strictly between them number b − a − 1.

Order

Write the names in a line as soon as a comparison is given. A is older than B means A, then B, if “left” means older.

Ages

Translate “three times” and “after 12 years” into equations. The future age of each person increases by the same number of years.

Common traps

p + q counts the person twice. Subtract 1.

People between two ranks are not b − a. Subtract one more.

“A is older than B, and C is younger than B” puts C at the young end only if no other person is mentioned.

In an age question, add the years to both people before you write the new relation.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
In a row, Ravi is 8th from the left and 15th from the right. How many people are in the row?

  1. 22
  2. 23
  3. 21
  4. 30

Solution

  1. Ravi is counted in both ranks.
  2. Total = 8 + 15 − 1 = 22.

Answer: A. 7 people stand to his left and 14 to his right. 7+1+14 = 22.

Example 2
A is 10th from the left and B is 15th from the left in the same row. How many people stand strictly between them?

  1. 4
  2. 5
  3. 6
  4. 25

Solution

  1. The positions between 10 and 15 are 11, 12, 13 and 14.
  2. That is 15 − 10 − 1 = 4.

Answer: A. Five would be the gap if one of the two people were included.

Example 3
A is older than B. C is younger than B. Who is the youngest?

  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
  4. Cannot be decided

Solution

  1. A is older than B, and B is older than C.
  2. C is younger than both.
  3. C is the youngest.

Answer: C. No fourth person is in the question.

Example 4
A father is three times as old as his son. In 12 years he will be twice as old as his son. The son’s present age is

  1. 8 years
  2. 10 years
  3. 12 years
  4. 16 years

Solution

  1. Let the son’s age be s. The father is 3s.
  2. After 12 years: 3s + 12 = 2(s + 12).
  3. 3s + 12 = 2s + 24, so s = 12.

Answer: C. The father is 36 now, and in 12 years the ages 48 and 24 are in the ratio 2:1.

P is taller than Q. R is taller than P. The tallest is

  1. P
  2. Q
  3. R
  4. Cannot be decided
Show answer

Answer: C. R is taller than P, who is taller than Q.

Practice 4
The sum of the ages of a mother and daughter is 50. The mother is 30 years older than the daughter. The daughter is

  1. 10
  2. 15
  3. 20
  4. 25
Show answer

Answer: A. If the daughter is d, the mother is d+30, and 2d + 30 = 50, so d = 10.

Practice 5
In a row of 40, A is 12th from the left. A’s position from the right is

  1. 28th
  2. 29th
  3. 30th
  4. 27th
Show answer

Answer: B. 40 − 12 + 1 = 29.

Practice 6
Five years ago A was twice as old as B. Their present ages add to 40. A is now

  1. 25
  2. 20
  3. 30
  4. 15
Show answer

Answer: A. A − 5 = 2(B − 5) gives A = 2B − 5. With A + B = 40, B = 15 and A = 25.

Practice 7
A and B are 7th and 14th from the left. The number of people strictly between them is

  1. 7
  2. 5
  3. 6
  4. 21
Show answer

Answer: C. 14 − 7 − 1 = 6.

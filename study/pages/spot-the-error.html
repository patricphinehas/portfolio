English · Tier-I and Tier-II English

Spot the error

Error spotting tests whether a sentence follows ordinary written English. The usual faults are the verb not matching the subject, a wrong tense, a wrong preposition, or a double comparative.

Read the subject and the verb first. Then read the time words: last year, since, yesterday. Those words often decide the tense.

What SSC lists

  • Spot the error in a sentence.
  • Subject-verb agreement, tense, articles and prepositions.
  • The same rules return inside sentence improvement and cloze tests.
  • Tier-II lists this item directly. Tier-I tests the same sense of correct English.

Rules and formulas

Agreement

A singular subject takes a singular verb. He does, not he do.
One of the boys takes a plural noun and a singular verb: one of the boys has passed.
Words joined by “along with” or “as well as” keep the verb of the main subject: The teacher, along with the students, is here.

Tense clues

Yesterday, last year and ago take the simple past: I visited Delhi last year.
Do not use the present perfect with a finished time word.

Fixed pairs

Senior to, junior to, prefer to, different from.
Discuss the plan, not discuss about the plan.
Return, not return back. Better, not more better.

Nouns that look plural

News, mathematics and physics take a singular verb: the news is good.

Common traps

“One of the boys” is singular in the verb. The noun after of is plural.

A finished time word does not sit with “have visited”.

More better, return back and discuss about are double forms.

The news is singular even though the word ends in s.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
Which sentence is correct?

  1. He do not know the answer.
  2. He does not know the answer.
  3. He does not knows the answer.
  4. He not knows the answer.

Solution

  1. The subject He is singular.
  2. The auxiliary is does, and the main verb stays know.
  3. Does not knows adds a second -s.

Answer: B. In the simple present, the -s belongs to the auxiliary in a negative sentence.

Example 2
Which sentence is correct?

  1. One of the boys have passed.
  2. One of the boy has passed.
  3. One of the boys has passed.
  4. One of boys have passed.

Solution

  1. One is the subject, so the verb is singular: has.
  2. The noun after of is plural: boys.
  3. Both of those facts appear together only in option C.

Answer: C. “One of the boy” fails the noun. “Have” fails the verb.

Example 3
Which sentence is correct?

  1. She is senior than me.
  2. She is senior to me.
  3. She is senior from me.
  4. She is more senior than from me.

Solution

  1. Senior, junior, superior, inferior and prefer take to, not than.
  2. The correct sentence is “senior to me”.

Answer: B. Than belongs with a comparative such as older, not with senior.

Example 4
Which sentence is correct?

  1. I have visited Delhi last year.
  2. I visited Delhi last year.
  3. I have visit Delhi last year.
  4. I am visiting Delhi last year.

Solution

  1. Last year names a finished time.
  2. The simple past is visited.
  3. The present perfect does not take last year.

Answer: B. “I have visited Delhi” is fine only when no finished time is attached.

Example 5
Which sentence is correct?

  1. The news are encouraging.
  2. The news is encouraging.
  3. The news were encouraging today as a plural subject.
  4. News are encouraging.

Solution

  1. News is treated as a singular noun.
  2. The verb is is.

Answer: B. The same habit applies to mathematics and physics in this test.

Example 6
(CGL pattern) Identify the segment with an error: The shop is open / from 9 a.m. / still 9 p.m. / on weekdays.

  1. The shop is open
  2. from 9 a.m.
  3. still 9 p.m.
  4. on weekdays

Solution

  1. The pair is from … till (or until).
  2. Still is an adverb of time, not a preposition here.

Answer: C. From … to and from … till are the correct pairs.

  2. The teacher, along with the students, is late.
  3. The teacher along with the students have late.
  4. The teachers, along with the student, is late if one teacher is meant.

Answer: B. The main subject is the teacher, singular, so the verb is is.

  1. He discussed about the budget.
  2. He discussed the budget.
  3. He discussed on the budget.
  4. He discussed for the budget.
Show answer

Answer: B. Discuss takes a direct object and no about.

Practice 3

  1. This pen is more better.
  2. This pen is better.
  3. This pen is more better than that.
  4. This pen is best than that.
Show answer

Answer: B. Better is already the comparative. Do not add more.

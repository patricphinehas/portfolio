English · Tier-I and Tier-II English

Active and passive voice

In the active voice, the subject does the action. In the passive voice, the subject receives the action. The tense does not change. The verb becomes a form of be plus the past participle.

“The cat killed the mouse” and “The mouse was killed by the cat” tell the same event. Killed is simple past, so the passive uses was, not is.

What SSC lists

  • Active and passive voice of verbs.
  • The tense-wise change of the verb.
  • Questions and requests.
  • Sentences where by plus the doer can be dropped if the doer is unknown.

Rules and formulas

The shape

Active: subject + verb + object.
Passive: object + be + past participle + by + doer.
The tense of be matches the tense of the original verb.

Tense map

Simple present: writes → is/are written.
Present continuous: is writing → is being written.
Present perfect: has written → has been written.
Simple past: wrote → was/were written.
Past continuous: was writing → was being written.
Will write → will be written.
Can write → can be written.

Questions

Who wrote this letter? → By whom was this letter written?

Requests

Please open the door → You are requested to open the door.
A bare order, Open the door, can become Let the door be opened.

Common traps

The tense must not jump. A past active does not become a present passive.

Continuous passives need being: is being written, not is written, for “is writing”.

Perfect passives need been: has been written.

Do not add by him if the original sentence has no doer and the option without it is correct.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
The cat killed the mouse. The passive form is

  1. The mouse is killed by the cat.
  2. The mouse was killed by the cat.
  3. The mouse has killed by the cat.
  4. The mouse was being killed by the cat.

Solution

  1. Killed is simple past.
  2. The passive uses was plus the past participle killed.

Answer: B. Is killed would be simple present. Was being killed would be past continuous.

Example 2
She is writing a letter. The passive form is

  1. A letter is written by her.
  2. A letter is being written by her.
  3. A letter has been written by her.
  4. A letter was written by her.

Solution

  1. Is writing is present continuous.
  2. The passive is is being written.

Answer: B. Being is the mark of the continuous passive.

Example 3
They have completed the work. The passive form is

  1. The work has completed by them.
  2. The work has been completed by them.
  3. The work is completed by them.
  4. The work had completed by them.

Solution

  1. Have completed is present perfect.
  2. The passive is has been completed.

Answer: B. Been is required. Has completed alone is still active in form.

Example 4
Who wrote this letter? The passive form is

  1. By whom this letter was written?
  2. By whom was this letter written?
  3. Who was this letter written?
  4. By who the letter was written?

Solution

  1. The question keeps a question order.
  2. By whom was this letter written?

Answer: B. Whom is the object form, and the auxiliary was comes before the subject.

Example 5
Please help me. Said as a request, the passive idea is

  1. You are requested to help me.
  2. Let me helped.
  3. I am help by you.
  4. You are requesting to help.

Solution

  1. A polite request becomes “You are requested to…”.
  2. The rest of the sentence keeps the action help me.

Answer: A. This is the form these papers expect for please.

Example 6
(CGL pattern) The council will open the bridge next week, and they are already testing it. The passive form is

  1. The bridge was opened by the council next week, and it is already tested.
  2. The bridge will be opened by the council next week, and it is already being tested.
  3. The bridge will have been opened next week, and it was being tested.
  4. The bridge will opened by the council, and it already being tested.

Solution

  1. Will open → will be opened.
  2. Are testing → is being tested.
  3. Each clause keeps its own tense.

Answer: B. Compound sentences convert one clause at a time.

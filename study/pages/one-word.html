English · Tier-I and Tier-II English

One-word substitution

One-word substitution gives you a definition and asks for the single word. The definition in the question is often longer than the word. Match every part of the definition, not just the pleasant half.

“A life story written by the person himself or herself” is an autobiography. A biography is written by someone else. That one word of difference is the question.

What SSC lists

  • One-word substitution.
  • People, places, speech and time words that the exam repeats.
  • The contrast between a pair, such as optimist and pessimist.
  • Using the word, once you have it, in a short sentence of your own.

Rules and formulas

People

An optimist looks on the bright side. A pessimist expects the worst.
An illiterate person cannot read or write.
A person who can use both hands equally well is ambidextrous.

Books and speech

An autobiography is a life story written by the person concerned. A biography is written by someone else.
An extempore speech is made without preparation.

Time

Annual: once a year. Biennial: every two years.
Omnipresent: present everywhere. Invisible: that which cannot be seen.

Places

An apiary is a place where bees are kept.
A sanctuary is a place of refuge, including a reserve for animals in many exam items.

Phobias

Claustrophobia: closed spaces. Agoraphobia: open or crowded places. Acrophobia: heights. Hydrophobia: water. Xenophobia: foreigners. Photophobia: light.

Common traps

Autobiography and biography are not interchangeable.

Biennial is two years, not twice a year. Twice a year is biannual, and many papers avoid that pair because it is confusing. Read the definition slowly.

Illiterate is not the same as uneducated in the loose sense. The definition is about reading and writing.

Extempore is unprepared speech, not a long written essay.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
A person who looks on the bright side of things is an

  1. pessimist
  2. optimist
  3. atheist
  4. linguist

Solution

  1. The bright side is the hopeful view.
  2. The word is optimist.

Answer: B. A pessimist expects a bad outcome.

Example 2
A life story written by the person himself or herself is an

  1. biography
  2. autobiography
  3. memoir only in speech
  4. diary of another person

Solution

  1. The writer and the subject are the same person.
  2. The word is autobiography.

Answer: B. A biography is written by someone else.

Example 3
A speech made without preparation is

  1. extempore
  2. manuscript
  3. epilogue
  4. prologue

Solution

  1. Without preparation is the whole definition.
  2. The word is extempore.

Answer: A. A manuscript is a written text, the opposite of this idea.

Example 4
A person who cannot read or write is

  1. illegible
  2. illiterate
  3. innumerate only
  4. blind

Solution

  1. The definition is about the skill of reading and writing, not about eyesight.
  2. The word is illiterate.

Answer: B. Illegible describes handwriting that cannot be read, not the person.

Example 5
A place where bees are kept is an

  1. aviary
  2. apiary
  3. aquarium
  4. arsenal

Solution

  1. Bees, not birds.
  2. An apiary keeps bees. An aviary keeps birds.

Answer: B. The two words are close in sound and far apart in meaning.

Example 6
(CGL pattern) Fear of closed spaces

  1. Photophobia
  2. Claustrophobia
  3. Agoraphobia
  4. Hydrophobia

Solution

  1. Claustro- points to a closed place.
  2. Agoraphobia is the fear of open or crowded places.

Answer: B. Agoraphobia is the usual trap.

Example 7
(CGL pattern) A choice between two unsatisfactory alternatives

  1. Problem
  2. Dilemma
  3. Confusion
  4. Difficulty

Solution

  1. Di- means two.
  2. A dilemma is a forced choice between two poor options.

Answer: B. Problem and difficulty are too general.

Example 8
(CGL pattern) A short introduction to a book, often written by someone other than the author

  1. Foreword
  2. Postscript
  3. Epilogue
  4. Appendix

Solution

  1. It comes before the main text.
  2. Postscript and epilogue come after it.

Answer: A. A preface is usually by the author; a foreword often by someone else.

Practice yourself

Attempt these without opening the answer. Then check the toggle.

Practice 1
Present everywhere is

  1. omnipotent
  2. omnipresent
  3. omniscient
  4. obsolete
Show answer

Answer: B. Omnipresent means present in all places. Omnipotent means all-powerful.

Practice 2
Happening once a year is

  1. annual
  2. biennial
  3. monthly
  4. daily
Show answer

Answer: A. Annual comes from the idea of a year.

Practice 3
That which cannot be seen is

  1. invisible
  2. invaluable
  3. indivisible
  4. inflammable
Show answer

Answer: A. Invisible means not able to be seen. Invaluable means extremely useful, not “without value”.

Practice 4
A person who expects the worst is a

  1. optimist
  2. realist only
  3. pessimist
  4. philanthropist
Show answer

Answer: C. A pessimist takes the dark view. A philanthropist works for others’ welfare.

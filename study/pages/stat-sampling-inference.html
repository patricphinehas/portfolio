Sampling and inference

Sampling is how you pick the units. Inference is what you claim about the population after you have seen the sample. A parameter describes the population. A statistic describes the sample.

The syllabus asks for the named sampling designs, the properties of a good estimator, and the tests based on Z, t, chi-square and F. State the conditions. Do not invent a critical value you have not looked up.

What SSC lists

  • Population, sample, parameter and statistic.
  • Sampling and non-sampling errors, and the listed designs.
  • Point and interval estimation, and the properties of a good estimator.
  • Tests using Z, t, chi-square and F, including the idea of a confidence interval.

Rules and formulas

Designs

Probability designs include simple random, stratified, systematic, cluster, multistage and multiphase sampling.
Non-probability designs include purposive, convenience and quota sampling.
A sampling error comes from the fact of sampling. A non-sampling error comes from a bad frame, a wrong reply, or a processing mistake, and it can occur even in a census.

Estimators

Unbiased: the expected value of the estimator equals the parameter.
Consistent: as the sample grows, the estimator settles on the parameter.
Efficient: smaller variance among unbiased competitors.
Methods named in the syllabus: moments, maximum likelihood, and least squares.

Tests

A null hypothesis is the claim you test. A Type I error rejects a true null. A Type II error accepts a false null.
Z is the large-sample test when the standard deviation can be treated as known or the sample is large.
t is the small-sample test for a mean when the population standard deviation is unknown.
Chi-square is used for goodness of fit and for independence in a contingency table.
F is a ratio of variances, and it is the statistic of ANOVA.

Interval

A confidence interval is a range, built from the sample, that is constructed to cover the parameter with a stated confidence.
It is not a probability statement about the parameter after the interval has already been calculated, in the strict reading. For the exam, know that a 95% interval is wider than a 90% interval for the same data, and that a larger sample narrows it.

Quick methods

Standard error of the mean = σ/√n. Four times the sample halves the SE.
Large-sample intervals: 95% → x̄ ± 1.96 σ/√n. 99% → x̄ ± 2.58 σ/√n.
Z for a mean = (x̄ − μ)/(σ/√n).
Degrees of freedom: one-sample t, n − 1. Chi-square in an r × c table, (r − 1)(c − 1).
Systematic sampling interval k = N/n.

Common traps

A convenience sample is not a simple random sample.

Stratified sampling divides the population into groups and samples each group. Cluster sampling picks whole groups.

Type I and Type II errors are easy to swap. Type I is the false rejection.

t, not Z, is the small-sample choice when sigma is unknown.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
A parameter describes the

  1. sample
  2. population
  3. chart colour
  4. typewriter

Solution

  1. A parameter is a population quantity.
  2. The matching sample quantity is a statistic.

Answer: B. The sample mean is a statistic used to estimate the population mean.

Example 2
Picking whole villages and then surveying everyone in the picked villages is closest to

  1. cluster sampling
  2. quota sampling
  3. a pie chart
  4. a t table

Solution

  1. The village is the cluster.
  2. You select clusters, not individuals one by one from the whole list.

Answer: A. Quota sampling would fill categories without a random mechanism.

Example 3
Rejecting a true null hypothesis is a

  1. Type I error
  2. Type II error
  3. sampling frame
  4. confidence of 0

Solution

  1. The null was true and you rejected it.
  2. That is Type I.

Answer: A. Type II is failing to reject a false null.

Example 4
When the sample is small and the population standard deviation is unknown, a test for the mean uses

  1. t
  2. only a pie chart
  3. the range as a p-value
  4. Spearman’s coefficient by default

Solution

  1. Sigma unknown and n small is the t setting.
  2. Z wants a known sigma or a large n.

Answer: A. The degrees of freedom for a single mean are n − 1.

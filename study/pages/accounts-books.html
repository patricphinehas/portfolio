Books, errors and final accounts

The books are the path from a transaction to a balance sheet. A journal records the entry. A ledger sorts it by account. A trial balance checks that debits equal credits. The trading account finds gross profit. The profit and loss account finds net profit. The balance sheet lists what is owned and what is owed.

Depreciation, inventory, bills, non-profit accounts and bank reconciliation are all on the official list. Each one repairs a figure that would otherwise be wrong.

What SSC lists

  • Journal, ledger, trial balance and rectification of errors.
  • Manufacturing, trading and profit and loss accounts, and the balance sheet.
  • Depreciation, inventory, bills of exchange and non-profit accounts.
  • Bank reconciliation, starting from a favourable cash-book balance.

Rules and formulas

The path

Journal: the first book of entry, with a debit, a credit and a narration.
Ledger: one page, or account, for each item.
Trial balance: a list of balances. If the books are arithmetically correct, debit totals equal credit totals. Agreement does not catch every error. A wrong account on the correct side can still agree.

Results

Trading account: gross profit = net sales − cost of goods sold, in the simplest goods business.
Profit and loss account: gross profit, plus other income, minus operating expenses, gives net profit.
Balance sheet: assets on one side, liabilities and capital on the other. Net profit is added to capital. Drawings are deducted.

Depreciation and stock

Straight line: (cost − residual value) / useful life, the same amount each year.
Written-down value: a fixed percent of the opening book value, so the amount falls each year.
Inventory is commonly valued at cost or net realisable value, whichever is lower.
A bill of exchange is a written order to pay. The drawer writes it, the drawee accepts it, and the payee receives the money.

Bank reconciliation

Start from a favourable (debit) balance as per the cash book.
Add cheques issued but not yet presented.
Subtract cheques deposited but not yet credited.
Subtract bank charges not yet in the cash book. Add interest credited by the bank and not yet in the cash book.
You arrive at the balance as per the pass book. If you start from the pass book, the signs reverse.

Quick methods

Written-down value after n years = cost × (1 − r/100)ⁿ.
Cost of goods sold = opening stock + purchases + direct expenses − closing stock.
Gross profit = net sales − cost of goods sold.
Capital at end = capital at start + net profit + fresh capital − drawings.

Common traps

A trial balance that agrees can still hide an error of principle, such as treating a capital expense as revenue, if the debit and credit were equal.

Gross profit is not net profit. Expenses below the trading account still have to be deducted.

Straight line and written-down value do not give the same charge every year, except by accident.

Cheques issued but not presented have already been credited in the cash book and not yet in the pass book. You add them when you walk from the cash book to the pass book.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
A trial balance is prepared to check that

  1. every error of principle is impossible
  2. total debits equal total credits
  3. cash in hand is zero
  4. depreciation is always zero

Solution

  1. It is an arithmetic check.
  2. Equal totals do not prove that the right accounts were chosen.

Answer: B. An error of principle can leave the totals equal.

Example 2
A machine costs Rs 50000, residual value Rs 5000, life 5 years. Straight-line depreciation per year is

  1. Rs 9000
  2. Rs 10000
  3. Rs 11000
  4. Rs 50000

Solution

  1. Depreciable amount = 50000 − 5000 = 45000.
  2. Per year = 45000 / 5 = 9000.

Answer: A. Forgetting the residual value would give 10000, the trap.

Example 3
Gross profit is found in the

  1. trading account
  2. balance sheet only
  3. bank reconciliation
  4. journal narration

Solution

  1. The trading account matches sales with the cost of goods.
  2. The result is gross profit or gross loss.

Answer: A. The profit and loss account then produces the net figure.

Example 4
Cheques issued but not yet presented are, when you start from a favourable cash-book balance,

  1. added to reach the pass-book balance
  2. ignored
  3. always a bank charge
  4. subtracted twice

Solution

  1. The cash book has already reduced the balance.
  2. The bank has not yet paid the cheque, so the pass book is higher.
  3. Add those cheques.

Answer: A. Deposits not yet credited are the ones you subtract.

Practice yourself

Attempt these without opening the answer. Then check the toggle.

Practice 1
Written-down value depreciation is calculated on

  1. the original cost every year
  2. the opening book value
  3. the sales of the year
  4. drawings
Show answer

Answer: B. The percent is applied to the reducing book value.

Practice 2
Inventory is prudently valued at

  1. the higher of cost and selling price
  2. cost or net realisable value, whichever is lower
  3. always zero
  4. the owner’s estimate with no rule
Show answer

Answer: B. The lower of cost and net realisable value.

Practice 3
In a non-profit organisation, the income and expenditure account is closest to

  1. a receipts and payments account, which is a cash summary
  2. a profit and loss account
  3. a cheque book
  4. a trial balance of a bank only
Show answer

Answer: B. Income and expenditure is on accrual lines. Receipts and payments is the cash summary.

Practice 4
Net profit in the final accounts is

  1. deducted from capital
  2. added to capital
  3. a fictitious asset
  4. a current liability always
Show answer

Answer: B. It increases the owner’s capital. Drawings decrease it.

Practice 5
A machine costs Rs 10000 and is depreciated at 10% on written-down value. Its book value after 2 years is

  1. Rs 8000
  2. Rs 8100
  3. Rs 8200
  4. Rs 9000
Show answer

Answer: B. 10000 × 0.9 × 0.9 = 8100.

Practice 6
Opening stock 5000, purchases 20000, closing stock 3000, sales 30000. Gross profit is

  1. Rs 5000
  2. Rs 8000
  3. Rs 10000
  4. Rs 12000
Show answer

Answer: B. COGS = 5000 + 20000 − 3000 = 22000. GP = 30000 − 22000 = 8000.

Practice 7
Opening capital 50000, net profit 12000, drawings 4000, no fresh capital. Closing capital is

  1. Rs 46000
  2. Rs 58000
  3. Rs 62000
  4. Rs 66000
Show answer

Answer: B. 50000 + 12000 − 4000 = 58000.

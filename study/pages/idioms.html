English · Tier-I and Tier-II English

Idioms and phrases

An idiom means something other than the literal words. Once in a blue moon is not about the moon. It means very rarely. Learn the meaning as a whole phrase, then use it in a sentence so the meaning sticks.

The question usually gives the idiom and four meanings, or a meaning and four idioms.

What SSC lists

  • Idioms and phrases.
  • Matching an idiom to its meaning.
  • Using the idiom in a sentence without twisting it.
  • A small set of high-frequency idioms is enough to start; add to it from the passages you read.

Rules and formulas

Frequency and effort

Once in a blue moon: very rarely.
Burn the midnight oil: work late into the night.
A piece of cake: very easy.

Speech

Spill the beans: reveal a secret.
Let the cat out of the bag: reveal a secret.
Hit the nail on the head: say or do exactly the right thing.

Difficulty

Bite the bullet: face something unpleasant with courage.
A blessing in disguise: something that looked bad and turned out well.
Burn the candle at both ends: exhaust yourself by doing too much.

How to answer

Replace the idiom with your meaning inside the sentence. If the sentence still makes sense, keep that option.

Common traps

Do not choose the literal picture. A blue moon is not the answer.

Spill the beans and let the cat out of the bag are close. Read whether the option says “reveal a secret” or something narrower.

A blessing in disguise is not simply a blessing. The disguise, the bad appearance, is part of the meaning.

An idiom is fixed. Do not “improve” its grammar in the answer.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
Once in a blue moon means

  1. every month
  2. very rarely
  3. at night
  4. on a festival

Solution

  1. The phrase is an idiom for a rare event.
  2. The meaning is very rarely.

Answer: B. The moon is not being discussed.

Example 2
To bite the bullet means

  1. to eat quickly
  2. to face a difficulty bravely
  3. to win a fight
  4. to keep silent

Solution

  1. The idiom means accepting something painful and going on.
  2. Face a difficulty bravely.

Answer: B. There is no literal bullet in the sentence.

Example 3
To burn the midnight oil means

  1. to waste fuel
  2. to work late at night
  3. to cook a meal
  4. to start a fire

Solution

  1. The picture is of a lamp still burning late.
  2. The meaning is to study or work late.

Answer: B. The oil is the old lamp, not a cooking ingredient.

Example 4
A blessing in disguise means

  1. a hidden curse
  2. a bad event that turns out well
  3. a religious ceremony
  4. a secret gift of money

Solution

  1. Something looks unfortunate at first.
  2. It later proves to be good.

Answer: B. The disguise is the bad appearance.

Example 5
To hit the nail on the head means

  1. to do carpentry
  2. to be exactly right
  3. to hurt someone
  4. to miss the point

Solution

  1. The idiom means to identify the point exactly.
  2. To be exactly right.

Answer: B. Missing the point is closer to the opposite.

Quantitative Aptitude · Tier-I and Paper-I

Time and work

If a person finishes a job in n days, the person does 1/n of the job in one day. Add those daily fractions when people work together. Pipes and leaks are the same idea: a leak contributes a negative fraction.

When the question is about men and days, the total work is the number of man-days, and that product stays fixed if everyone works at the same rate.

What SSC lists

  • Time and work for one person and for two people together.
  • Work left after part of the job is done.
  • Pipes filling a tank and a leak emptying it.
  • Men, days and the same piece of work.

Rules and formulas

One worker

If A finishes in a days, A’s one-day work = 1/a.

Together

One-day work together = 1/a + 1/b.
Days needed = 1 / (that sum).

Pipes

A filling pipe is positive. An emptying leak is negative.
Net rate = fill rate − empty rate.

Men and days

If M1 men take D1 days, then M1 × D1 = M2 × D2 for the same work, when rates are equal.
If hours per day also change, include hours: M × D × H is constant.

Quick methods

LCM method: total work = LCM of the days. Each person’s rate = total/days. No fractions.
Two people: days together = ab/(a + b).
Together x days, A alone a days: B alone = ax/(a − x).
Efficiency and days are inverse: 25% more efficient means days × 100/125.
Wages are shared in the ratio of work done.

Common traps

“A is twice as fast as B” means A’s daily work is twice B’s, so A takes half as many days, not twice as many.

A leak is subtracted. It does not get added to the filling pipe.

After A works for 3 of his 12 days, the remaining work is 3/4, not 1/4.

Men × days uses the same work. Do not also divide by the wage unless the question is about wages.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
A can finish a work in 10 days and B in 15 days. Together they finish it in

  1. 5 days
  2. 6 days
  3. 8 days
  4. 12 days

Solution

  1. A’s one-day work = 1/10. B’s = 1/15.
  2. Together = 1/10 + 1/15 = 3/30 + 2/30 = 1/6.
  3. Days = 6.

Answer: B. In 6 days, A does 6/10 and B does 6/15, and 0.6 + 0.4 = 1.

Example 2
A can do a piece of work in 12 days and works alone for 3 days. The fraction of work still left is

  1. 1/4
  2. 1/2
  3. 3/4
  4. 1/3

Solution

  1. In one day A does 1/12.
  2. In 3 days A does 3/12 = 1/4.
  3. Work left = 1 − 1/4 = 3/4.

Answer: C. Three days is a quarter of A’s time, so three quarters remain.

Example 3
A is twice as good a workman as B. Together they finish a job in 12 days. A alone would finish it in

  1. 15 days
  2. 18 days
  3. 24 days
  4. 36 days

Solution

  1. Let B’s one-day work be k. Then A’s is 2k.
  2. Together 3k = 1/12, so k = 1/36.
  3. A’s one-day work is 2/36 = 1/18, so A alone takes 18 days.

Answer: B. B alone would take 36 days, which is the trap if you assign the rates backwards.

Example 4
A pipe fills a tank in 6 hours. A leak empties it in 12 hours. With both open, the tank fills in

  1. 6 hours
  2. 9 hours
  3. 12 hours
  4. 18 hours

Solution

  1. Fill rate = 1/6. Empty rate = 1/12.
  2. Net = 1/6 − 1/12 = 1/12.
  3. Time = 12 hours.

Answer: C. The leak removes half of what the pipe adds each hour.

Example 5
12 men can finish a work in 10 days. How many days will 8 men take, at the same rate?

  1. 12
  2. 15
  3. 16
  4. 18

Solution

  1. Total work = 12 × 10 = 120 man-days.
  2. Eight men take 120/8 = 15 days.

Answer: B. Fewer men take more days. The product stays 120.

Example 6
(CGL pattern) Pipes A and B fill a tank in 12 h and 18 h. Pipe C empties it in 36 h. A and B are opened at 8 a.m. on an empty tank, and C is opened at 10 a.m. The tank is full at

  1. 4:30 p.m.
  2. 3:30 p.m.
  3. 5:00 p.m.
  4. 2:30 p.m.

Solution

  1. Tank = LCM 36 units. A = 3, B = 2, C = −1 per hour.
  2. 8 to 10 a.m.: 2 × 5 = 10 units.
  3. Remaining 26 units at 4 per hour = 6.5 h after 10 a.m.

Answer: A. Handle the time before C opens as a separate block.

Practice yourself

Attempt these without opening the answer. Then check the toggle.

Practice 1
A completes a work in 8 days. A’s one-day work is

  1. 8
  2. 1/8
  3. 1/4
  4. 4
Show answer

Answer: B. The whole work is 1, so one day is 1/8.

Practice 2
A and B together finish a work in 4 days. A alone takes 6 days. B alone takes

  1. 8 days
  2. 10 days
  3. 12 days
  4. 24 days
Show answer

Answer: C. B’s rate = 1/4 − 1/6 = 1/12, so B takes 12 days.

Practice 3
6 men finish a job in 8 days. The man-days of work are

  1. 14
  2. 36
  3. 48
  4. 64
Show answer

Answer: C. 6 × 8 = 48 man-days.

Practice 4
A tap fills a tank in 4 hours and another fills it in 6 hours. Together they fill it in

  1. 2.4 hours
  2. 5 hours
  3. 10 hours
  4. 12 hours
Show answer

Answer: A. 1/4 + 1/6 = 5/12. Time = 12/5 = 2.4 hours.

Practice 5
A takes 20 days, B 30 days and C 60 days. Together they take

  1. 8 days
  2. 10 days
  3. 12 days
  4. 15 days
Show answer

Answer: B. Total work 60 units. Rates 3 + 2 + 1 = 6 per day, so 10 days.

Practice 6
A is 25% more efficient than B. B takes 20 days. A takes

  1. 16 days
  2. 15 days
  3. 18 days
  4. 25 days
Show answer

Answer: A. 20 × 100/125 = 16.

Practice 7
A takes 12 days and B 24 days. Together they earn Rs 1800 for the job. A’s share is

  1. Rs 600
  2. Rs 900
  3. Rs 1200
  4. Rs 1350
Show answer

Answer: C. Rates are 2:1, so A gets 2/3 of 1800 = 1200.

Practice 8
Pipe A fills a tank in 10 h, B in 15 h, and C empties it in 30 h. With all three open, it fills in

  1. 6 h
  2. 9 h
  3. 10 h
  4. 7.5 h
Show answer

Answer: D. Tank = 30 units. Net rate 3 + 2 − 1 = 4 per hour. 30/4 = 7.5 h.

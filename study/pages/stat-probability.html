Probability and distributions

Paper-II probability starts where the simple fraction of Paper-I stops. You need conditional probability, independence, Bayes’ theorem, and the four named distributions: binomial, Poisson, normal and exponential.

A random variable assigns a number to each outcome. Its expectation is the long-run average of that number.

What SSC lists

  • Definitions of probability, conditional probability, independence and Bayes’ theorem.
  • Random variables, expectation and variance.
  • Binomial, Poisson, normal and exponential distributions.
  • The joint distribution of two discrete random variables, at the level of a table.

Rules and formulas

Rules

0 ≤ P(A) ≤ 1. P(not A) = 1 − P(A).
P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A and B).
If A and B are independent, P(A and B) = P(A)P(B).
P(A|B) = P(A and B) / P(B), if P(B) is not zero.

Bayes

P(A|B) = P(B|A) P(A) / P(B).
P(B) is found by the law of total probability when A is one of several causes.

Distributions

Binomial: n independent trials, success probability p. P(X = k) = C(n, k) p^k (1−p)^(n−k). Mean np. Variance np(1−p).
Poisson: P(X = k) = e^(−λ) λ^k / k!. Mean and variance are both λ.
Normal: symmetric, and mean = median = mode. The empirical rule is about 68%, 95% and 99.7% within 1, 2 and 3 standard deviations.
Exponential: a continuous waiting-time distribution with mean 1/λ if the rate is λ. Its variance is 1/λ².

Expectation

For a discrete variable, E(X) = Σ x P(X = x).
Var(X) = E(X²) − [E(X)]².

Quick methods

Total probability: P(B) = Σ P(Aᵢ) P(B|Aᵢ). Bayes divides one term by this total.
Binomial: P(X ≥ 1) = 1 − (1 − p)ⁿ.
Poisson: P(X = 0) = e^(−λ).
Standardise: Z = (X − μ)/σ.
E(aX + b) = aE(X) + b. Var(aX + b) = a² Var(X).

Common traps

Independent events are not the same as mutually exclusive events. Mutually exclusive events cannot happen together, so they are dependent unless one has probability zero.

Poisson mean equals Poisson variance. Binomial variance is smaller than its mean unless p is 0.

The normal curve is symmetric. A skewed histogram is not a reason to force a normal model.

Bayes needs the total probability in the denominator, not only the one cause you like.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
If P(A) = 0.3 and A and B are independent with P(B) = 0.5, then P(A and B) is

  1. 0.8
  2. 0.15
  3. 0.2
  4. 0.02

Solution

  1. Independence lets you multiply.
  2. 0.3 × 0.5 = 0.15.

Answer: B. 0.8 would be a mistaken addition.

Example 2
A fair coin is tossed 3 times. The probability of exactly 2 heads is

  1. 1/8
  2. 3/8
  3. 1/2
  4. 3/4

Solution

  1. This is binomial, n = 3, p = 1/2.
  2. C(3, 2) × (1/2)³ = 3/8.

Answer: B. Exactly two heads is not “at least two”, which would add the three-head case and become 4/8.

Example 3
For a Poisson variable with λ = 4, the variance is

  1. 2
  2. 4
  3. 8
  4. 16

Solution

  1. Mean and variance are both λ.
  2. Variance = 4.

Answer: B. The standard deviation would be 2, which is the square root.

Example 4
Box 1 has 2 red and 1 blue. Box 2 has 1 red and 2 blue. A box is chosen at random and then a ball, which is red. The probability it came from box 1 is

  1. 1/2
  2. 1/3
  3. 2/3
  4. 3/4

Solution

  1. P(red) = (1/2)×(2/3) + (1/2)×(1/3) = 1/2.
  2. P(box 1 and red) = (1/2)×(2/3) = 1/3.
  3. Bayes: (1/3) / (1/2) = 2/3.

Answer: C. The red ball is more likely from the box that is richer in red.

  1. 2
  2. 4
  3. 6
  4. 10
Show answer

Answer: B. np = 4. The variance would be 10 × 0.4 × 0.6 = 2.4.

Practice 3
For a normal distribution, mean, median and mode are

  1. all different
  2. equal
  3. in the ratio 1:2:3
  4. undefined
Show answer

Answer: B. Symmetry puts them at the same point.

Practice 4
Two events that cannot happen together are called

  1. independent
  2. mutually exclusive
  3. normal
  4. continuous
Show answer

Answer: B. Mutually exclusive. Independence is a different idea.

Practice 5
X is normal with mean 50 and SD 5. The Z value for X = 60 is

  1. 1
  2. 2
  3. 10
  4. 0.5
Show answer

Answer: B. (60 − 50)/5 = 2.

Practice 6
Var(X) = 4. Var(3X + 2) is

  1. 12
  2. 14
  3. 36
  4. 38
Show answer

Answer: C. 3² × 4 = 36. The +2 does not matter.

Practice 7
Binomial with n = 4, p = 1/2. P(X ≥ 1) is

  1. 1/16
  2. 1/2
  3. 15/16
  4. 3/4
Show answer

Answer: C. 1 − (1/2)⁴ = 15/16.

Practice 8

  1. 7
  2. 10
  3. 13
  4. 2

Quantitative Aptitude · Tier-I and Paper-I

Mensuration

Mensuration turns a shape into a length, an area or a volume. The syllabus runs from triangles and circles to the standard solids: cuboid, prism, pyramid, cylinder, cone, sphere and hemisphere.

Use one value of π inside a question. When the radius is a multiple of 7, 22/7 is the value that cancels. Write the unit: area is square, volume is cubic.

What SSC lists

  • Areas of triangle, quadrilateral, regular polygon and circle.
  • Right prism and right pyramid.
  • Right circular cylinder and cone.
  • Sphere, hemisphere and rectangular parallelepiped, which is a cuboid.

Rules and formulas

Plane figures

Triangle: (1/2)×base×height. Heron: √[s(s−a)(s−b)(s−c)].
Equilateral: (√3 / 4) × side².
Circle: area πr², circumference 2πr.
Use π = 22/7 when it cancels, otherwise leave π in the answer if the options do.

Cuboid and prism

Cuboid volume = length × breadth × height.
Total surface = 2(lb + bh + hl).
Prism volume = area of base × height.

Cylinder, cone, sphere

Cylinder: volume πr²h, curved surface 2πrh, total surface 2πr(r+h).
Cone: volume (1/3)πr²h, curved surface πrl, with slant l = √(r² + h²).
Sphere: volume (4/3)πr³, surface 4πr².
Hemisphere: curved surface 2πr², total surface 3πr², volume (2/3)πr³.

Pyramid

Volume of a pyramid = (1/3) × base area × height.
A square base of side a gives volume (1/3) × a² × h.

Quick methods

Square: diagonal a√2. Rectangle: diagonal √(l² + b²).
Rhombus: area = d₁ × d₂ / 2. Trapezium: area = ½ × (sum of parallel sides) × height.
Cube: diagonal a√3, total surface 6a². Cuboid diagonal √(l² + b² + h²).
Sector of angle θ: area = (θ/360) × πr², arc = (θ/360) × 2πr.
Scale every length by k: area × k², volume × k³.

Rolled sheets and sectors

A sheet rolled along one side: that side becomes the circumference (2πr), the other side becomes the height.
Sector: arc = (θ/360) × 2πr. Area = ½ × arc × r. Perimeter = arc + 2r.

Common traps

Hemisphere curved surface is 2πr². Total surface adds the circular base and becomes 3πr².

Cone volume has the factor 1/3. Cylinder volume does not.

Slant height of a cone is not the vertical height.

A prism’s volume uses the base area. Do not multiply all three edges unless the base is a rectangle and you have already found that area.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
The area of a circle of radius 7 cm, taking π = 22/7, is

  1. 154 cm²
  2. 44 cm²
  3. 308 cm²
  4. 616 cm²

Solution

  1. Area = πr² = (22/7) × 49.
  2. 22 × 7 = 154.

Answer: A. The circumference would be 2 × 22/7 × 7 = 44 cm, which is option B.

Example 2
The curved surface area of a hemisphere of radius 7 cm, with π = 22/7, is

  1. 154 cm²
  2. 308 cm²
  3. 462 cm²
  4. 616 cm²

Solution

  1. Curved surface = 2πr².
  2. 2 × (22/7) × 49 = 2 × 154 = 308.

Answer: B. The total surface, including the base, is 3πr² = 462 cm². That is the next trap.

Example 3
The volume of a cylinder of radius 7 cm and height 10 cm, with π = 22/7, is

  1. 1540 cm³
  2. 440 cm³
  3. 770 cm³
  4. 3080 cm³

Solution

  1. Volume = πr²h = (22/7) × 49 × 10.
  2. 22 × 7 × 10 = 1540.

Answer: A. Curved surface would be 2πrh = 440 cm², a different measure.

Example 4
A cone has radius 7 cm and height 24 cm. Its slant height is

  1. 25 cm
  2. 31 cm
  3. 28 cm
  4. 17 cm

Solution

  1. l = √(r² + h²) = √(49 + 576).
  2. √625 = 25 cm.

Answer: A. This is another 7-24-25 right triangle. Curved surface would then be π × 7 × 25 = 550 cm² if π = 22/7.

Example 5
The total surface area of a cuboid 5 cm × 4 cm × 3 cm is

  1. 47 cm²
  2. 60 cm²
  3. 94 cm²
  4. 120 cm²

Solution

  1. Volume would be 5 × 4 × 3 = 60.
  2. Pairs of faces: 5×4 = 20, 4×3 = 12, 5×3 = 15. Sum of the three pairs = 47.
  3. Total surface = 2 × 47 = 94 cm².

Answer: C. 60 is the volume. 47 is only half the surface.

Example 6
(CGL pattern) A sheet 44 cm by 20 cm is rolled along its 44 cm side to form a cylinder. With π = 22/7, its volume is

  1. 3080 cm³
  2. 1540 cm³
  3. 6160 cm³
  4. 2200 cm³

Solution

  1. 2πr = 44, so r = 7.
  2. Height = 20.
  3. V = 22/7 × 49 × 20 = 3080.

Answer: A. Rolling along the other side gives a different cylinder, so read which side curls.

Example 7
(CGL pattern) A sector of radius 10 cm has area 60 cm². Its perimeter is

  1. 22 cm
  2. 32 cm
  3. 26 cm
  4. 42 cm

Solution

  1. Area = ½ × arc × r, so arc = 2 × 60 / 10 = 12.
  2. Perimeter = 12 + 2 × 10 = 32.

Answer: B. Forgetting the two radii gives 12, and adding only one gives 22.

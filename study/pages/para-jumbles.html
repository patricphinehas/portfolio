English · Tier-I and Tier-II English

Para jumbles

A para jumble gives you three or four sentences in the wrong order. You rebuild the paragraph. Look for the sentence that can start: it does not begin with a pronoun pointing backwards, and it does not begin with “then” or “as a result”.

After a starting sentence, the next one should pick up a name or an idea the first one introduced.

What SSC lists

  • Shuffling of sentence parts, and shuffling of sentences in a passage.
  • Opening sentence, pronouns, and result words.
  • A short paragraph of three or four sentences.
  • The same attention to connectors helps the cloze test.

Rules and formulas

The opening

A good opening names the person or the event. It does not start with he, she, it or this unless the noun is inside the same sentence.

Connectors

Then, next, as a result, therefore and however point backwards. They cannot be sentence one.

Pronouns

If sentence B says “she”, an earlier sentence must have introduced the woman.

Check the chain

Read your order aloud. If a sentence answers a question the previous sentence has not asked, move it.

Common traps

A sentence starting with “Then” is rarely the opening.

A pronoun with no earlier noun is a broken order.

“As a result” follows the cause. It does not precede it.

Two orders may both be grammatical. Prefer the one where each sentence has a reason to follow the one before it.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
Arrange: P. Then she checked the answers. Q. Meera opened the practice set. R. She marked the two she had got wrong. S. First she solved every question without looking.

  1. QSPR
  2. PQRS
  3. SQPR
  4. QRSP

Solution

  1. Q names Meera and the set, so it opens.
  2. S says what she did first.
  3. P follows with then she checked.
  4. R is the last action, marking the wrong ones.

Answer: A. The order is Q, S, P, R.

Example 2
Arrange: P. As a result, the road was closed. Q. Heavy rain fell all night. R. A tree fell across the road. S. The rain loosened the soil.

  1. QSRP
  2. PQRS
  3. RQSP
  4. SQRP

Solution

  1. Q states the cause, the rain.
  2. S says what the rain did to the soil.
  3. R gives the tree falling.
  4. P is the result, the closed road.

Answer: A. As a result cannot open the paragraph.

Example 3
Arrange: P. He returned it the next day. Q. Arun borrowed a pen from Lata. R. The pen wrote smoothly. S. He used it in the examination.

  1. QRSP
  2. QPSR
  3. PSRQ
  4. SQRP

Solution

  1. Q names Arun and the borrowing, so it opens.
  2. R describes the pen he had just borrowed.
  3. S says he used that pen in the examination.
  4. P, the return next day, is last.

Answer: A. The order is Q, R, S, P.

Example 4
Arrange: P. Finally the seeds were stored in a dry jar. Q. The farmer dried the seeds in the sun. R. He cleaned the seeds after the harvest. S. The harvest ended in March.

  1. SRQP
  2. SQRP
  3. RQSP
  4. PSRQ

Solution

  1. S opens with the finished harvest.
  2. R follows: he cleaned the seeds.
  3. Q is the drying.
  4. P is the last step, storage.

Answer: A. The order is S, R, Q, P. Finally cannot open the paragraph.

Example 5
(CGL pattern) Arrange the parts into a sentence: P. has changed the way / Q. The internet / R. we work and learn / S. in the last two decades

  1. QPRS
  2. PQRS
  3. QRPS
  4. SQPR

Solution

  1. Q is the subject.
  2. P gives the verb, R completes ‘the way’, S gives the time.

Answer: A. Find the subject first, then the verb that follows it.

Practice yourself

Attempt these without opening the answer. Then check the toggle.

Practice 1
Which sentence can open a paragraph?

  1. Then the shop closed.
  2. As a result, the price fell.
  3. A small shop stood at the end of the lane.
  4. She locked its door after that.
Show answer

Answer: C. Only C introduces the shop. The others point back to something already said.

Practice 2
“Therefore the match was abandoned” should come

  1. before any mention of rain
  2. after the sentence that gives the cause
  3. as the first sentence
  4. after a sentence about a different sport with no link
Show answer

Answer: B. Therefore marks a result.

Practice 3
If one sentence says “Neha missed the bus” and another says “She started walking”, the walking sentence comes

  1. first
  2. second
  3. either, with no change of meaning
  4. not at all
Show answer

Answer: B. She needs Neha to be named first.

Practice 4
A sentence beginning “However” is likely to

  1. open the story with a new noun
  2. contrast something just said
  3. give a person’s full name for the first time
  4. be a title
Show answer

Answer: B. However signals a contrast with the previous sentence.

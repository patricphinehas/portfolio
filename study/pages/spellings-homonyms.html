Spellings and homonyms

Spelling questions ask you to spot the word written wrongly, or to pick the correct spelling from four close forms. Homonyms are a different trap: two words sound alike, or nearly alike, and only one means what the sentence says.

Say the word in a phrase. “Separate the papers” fixes the spelling better than staring at seperate.

What SSC lists

  • Spellings and detecting misspelt words.
  • Homonyms and words often confused.
  • Correct usage of the pair inside a sentence.
  • These items are listed in the Tier-II English syllabus and appear, in shorter form, in Tier-I.

Rules and formulas

Spellings that slip

Accommodate: two c’s, two m’s.
Occurrence: two c’s, two r’s. Committee: two m’s, two t’s.
Separate has an a in the second syllable, not an e. Definitely has no a.
Privilege, maintenance and pronunciation are worth writing out once.

Affect and effect

Affect is usually the verb: the news affected him.
Effect is usually the noun: the effect of the news. “Effect a change” uses effect as a verb meaning bring about, which is less common in this test.

Other pairs

Principal: the head, or the main sum. Principle: a rule.
Stationary: not moving. Stationery: paper and pens.
Complement: something that completes. Compliment: praise.
Desert (the dry land) and dessert (the sweet course) differ in spelling and stress.

A test

If you can swap in “praise”, the word is compliment. If you can swap in “complete”, it is complement.

Common traps

Seperate, definately, occurrance and commitee are the wrong spellings.

Effect is not the verb in “The delay will affect the result”.

Stationery is the paper. A stationary train is still.

The principal of a college is a person. A principle is an idea.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
Which spelling is correct?

  1. accomodate
  2. acommodate
  3. accommodate
  4. accommodete

Solution

  1. The word has two c’s and two m’s.
  2. Accommodate is the correct spelling.

Answer: C. Say it as ac-comm-o-date while you write it.

Example 2
Which spelling is correct?

  1. seperate
  2. separate
  3. seperete
  4. separat

Solution

  1. The second vowel is a.
  2. Separate is correct.

Answer: B. There is a rat in separate: sep-a-rat-e, as a memory line.

Example 3
The delay will ______ the result.

  1. effect
  2. affect
  3. effet
  4. afect

Solution

  1. The sentence needs a verb meaning influence.
  2. That verb is affect.

Answer: B. Effect would be the noun: the effect of the delay.

Example 4
Please buy some ______ for the office.

  1. stationary
  2. stationery
  3. stationry
  4. stasionery

Solution

  1. The office needs paper and pens.
  2. That noun is stationery, with an e.

Answer: B. Stationary, with an a, means not moving.

Example 5
The ______ of the college addressed the students.

  1. principle
  2. principal
  3. principel
  4. prinicpal

Solution

  1. The head of the college is the principal.
  2. Principle would mean a moral rule, which does not address students.

Answer: B. Both the person and the main sum of money use principal.

Example 6
(CGL pattern) The ______ of the parcel was 2 kg.

  1. wait
  2. weight
  3. white
  4. wide

Solution

  1. The sentence needs a noun meaning heaviness.
  2. Weight sounds like wait but means mass.

Answer: B. Homophones are the trap in this question type.

Example 7
(CGL pattern) Select the incorrectly spelt word.

  1. Necessary
  2. Embarrass
  3. Occasion
  4. Recieve

Solution

  1. I before e, except after c: receive.
  2. The other three are correct.

Answer: D. Necessary: one c, two s. Embarrass: two r, two s.

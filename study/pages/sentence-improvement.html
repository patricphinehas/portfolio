Sentence improvement

Sentence improvement gives you a sentence with one part underlined in the real paper, and asks for a better phrase. On this page the weak part is the whole sentence, and you choose the improved form. The improved form should fix the error and change nothing else.

If the sentence is already correct, a real paper often has “no improvement”. When that option is absent, one of the four sentences is already the good one.

What SSC lists

  • Improvement of sentences.
  • Removing double forms and wrong prepositions.
  • Repairing agreement and tense without rewriting the meaning.
  • The same errors as spot-the-error, now in the form of a correction.

Rules and formulas

Delete the extra word

More better → better. Return back → return. Discuss about → discuss.
Suppose if → if, or suppose, but not both.

Repair the pattern

Prefer tea to coffee, not prefer tea than coffee.
Scarcely had he arrived when the train left. Hardly and scarcely pair with when in this pattern; no sooner pairs with than.

Keep the meaning

An improvement that changes who did the action is not an improvement. It is a different sentence.

Already correct

If three options introduce a new error, the original wording is the answer.

Common traps

Do not “improve” a sentence by making it longer.

No sooner takes than, not when.

Prefer does not take than.

A correction of grammar that reverses the meaning fails the question.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
Choose the improved sentence. Original sense: he likes tea more than coffee.

  1. He prefers tea than coffee.
  2. He prefers tea to coffee.
  3. He prefers tea from coffee.
  4. He is preferring tea than coffee.

Solution

  1. Prefer takes to.
  2. The improved sentence is “He prefers tea to coffee”.

Answer: B. Than is the error the question is built to catch.

Example 2
Choose the improved sentence.

  1. This method is more better.
  2. This method is better.
  3. This method is more better than all.
  4. This method is best than that.

Solution

  1. Better must stand alone as the comparative.
  2. Drop more.

Answer: B. Best is a superlative and does not take than in this way.

Example 3
Choose the improved sentence.

  1. She returned back to the office.
  2. She returned to the office.
  3. She returned back from to the office.
  4. She had returned back.

Solution

  1. Return already means go back.
  2. The extra back is removed.

Answer: B. The tense does not need to change to repair this error.

Example 4
Choose the improved sentence.

  1. No sooner had he left when the rain started.
  2. No sooner had he left than the rain started.
  3. No sooner he had left than the rain started.
  4. No sooner had he left then the rain started.

Solution

  1. No sooner pairs with than.
  2. The auxiliary had stays before the subject in this pattern: had he left.

Answer: B. Option C repairs than and breaks the inversion.

  2. He discussed the plan.
  3. He discussed on the plan.
  4. He discussed for the plan.

Answer: B. Discuss takes the object directly.

Practice 2
Choose the better sentence.

  1. Suppose if it rains, we will wait.
  2. If it rains, we will wait.
  3. Suppose if it will rain, we wait.
  4. If suppose it rains, we will wait.
Show answer

Answer: B. Use if once. Do not add suppose as well.

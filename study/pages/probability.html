Quantitative Aptitude · Tier-I and Paper-I

Simple probability

Probability measures how many of the equally likely results are the ones you want. For Paper-I it stays with coins, dice, cards and a small bag of balls. The value is always from 0 to 1.

Paper-II, for the statistics posts, goes on to Bayes, binomial and normal distributions. Those pages are separate. Finish this page first.

What SSC lists

  • Calculation of simple probabilities.
  • A single coin, die or card.
  • Two dice, or two draws, when the question says whether the first item is replaced.
  • The probability that an event does not happen.

Rules and formulas

Definition

P(event) = (number of favourable results) / (number of equally likely results).
0 means impossible and 1 means certain.

Complement

P(not E) = 1 − P(E).

Dice and coins

One fair die has 6 faces. Two dice have 36 equally likely pairs.
One fair coin has 2 results. Two coins have 4: HH, HT, TH, TT.

Cards and draws

A standard deck has 52 cards, 4 suits, 13 ranks, and 4 aces.
Without replacement, the second draw has a smaller total.
With replacement, the probabilities multiply unchanged.

Quick methods

At least one: P = 1 − P(none).
Independent events multiply. Mutually exclusive events add.
n coins: 2ⁿ outcomes. n dice: 6ⁿ outcomes.
Cards: 26 red, 26 black, 12 face cards (J, Q, K), 4 of each rank.
Odds in favour a:b means P = a/(a + b).

Common traps

Two dice give 36 results, not 12.

“A king or a queen” adds the two counts if a card cannot be both. Do not subtract an overlap that does not exist.

Without replacement, the denominator changes on the second draw.

A probability greater than 1 means the counts were not of equally likely results.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
The probability of getting a prime number in one throw of a fair die is

  1. 1/6
  2. 1/3
  3. 1/2
  4. 2/3

Solution

  1. The faces are 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6.
  2. The primes are 2, 3 and 5.
  3. Probability = 3/6 = 1/2.

Answer: C. 1 is not a prime number.

Example 2
Two fair coins are tossed. The probability of two heads is

  1. 1/4
  2. 1/2
  3. 1/3
  4. 3/4

Solution

  1. The results are HH, HT, TH, TT.
  2. Only HH is favourable.
  3. Probability = 1/4.

Answer: A. “At least one head” would be 3/4. The question asks for both heads.

Example 3
One card is drawn from a standard pack of 52. The probability that it is an ace is

  1. 1/52
  2. 1/26
  3. 1/13
  4. 4/13

Solution

  1. There are 4 aces.
  2. Probability = 4/52 = 1/13.

Answer: C. The chance of a heart is 13/52 = 1/4, a different question.

Example 4
Two fair dice are thrown. The probability that the sum is 7 is

  1. 1/12
  2. 1/9
  3. 1/6
  4. 1/36

Solution

  1. There are 36 pairs.
  2. The pairs that sum to 7 are (1,6), (2,5), (3,4), (4,3), (5,2), (6,1): six pairs.
  3. Probability = 6/36 = 1/6.

Answer: C. A sum of 2 has only one pair, so its probability is 1/36.

Example 5
A bag has 3 red balls and 2 blue balls. Two balls are drawn together. The probability that both are red is

  1. 3/10
  2. 3/5
  3. 1/10
  4. 2/5

Solution

  1. Ways to choose 2 red out of 3 = 3.
  2. Ways to choose any 2 out of 5 = 10.
  3. Probability = 3/10.
  4. The same result is (3/5) × (2/4) = 3/10.

Answer: A. Drawing together is sampling without replacement, and order does not create a new pair.

Practice yourself

Practice 1
The probability of an impossible event is

  1. 0
  2. 1/2
  3. 1
  4. 2
Show answer

Answer: A. Nothing in the list of results counts as favourable.

Practice 2
A fair die is thrown. The probability of getting a number greater than 4 is

  1. 1/6
  2. 1/3
  3. 1/2
  4. 2/3
Show answer

Answer: B. The faces 5 and 6 work, so 2/6 = 1/3.

Practice 3
P(E) = 0.3. Then P(not E) is

  1. 0.3
  2. 0.5
  3. 0.7
  4. 1.3
Show answer

Answer: C. 1 − 0.3 = 0.7.

Practice 4
One card is drawn from 52 cards. The probability that it is a king or a queen is

  1. 1/13
  2. 2/13
  3. 4/52
  4. 1/26
Show answer

Answer: B. There are 4 kings and 4 queens, so 8/52 = 2/13.

Practice 5
Three coins are tossed. The probability of at least one head is

  1. 1/8
  2. 3/8
  3. 7/8
  4. 1/2
Show answer

Answer: C. 1 − P(TTT) = 1 − 1/8 = 7/8.

Practice 6
A card drawn from 52 is a face card with probability

  1. 3/13
  2. 1/13
  3. 4/13
  4. 3/52
Show answer

Answer: A. 12/52 = 3/13.

Practice 7
The odds in favour of an event are 2:3. Its probability is

  1. 2/3
  2. 3/5
  3. 2/5
  4. 1/3
Show answer

Answer: C. 2/(2 + 3) = 2/5.

Practice 8
Two dice are thrown. The probability of a doublet (both faces equal) is

  1. 1/36
  2. 1/12
  3. 1/6
  4. 1/3
Show answer

Answer: C. 6 doublets out of 36 = 1/6.

Practice 9
A die is thrown twice. The probability of two sixes is

  1. 1/6
  2. 1/12
  3. 1/36
  4. 1/3
Show answer

Answer: C. 1/6 × 1/6 = 1/36.

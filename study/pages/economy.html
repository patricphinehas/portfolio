General Awareness · Tier-I and Paper-I General Awareness

Indian economy

The economy questions in General Awareness are about institutions and definitions, not about last quarter’s GDP. Learn what GDP means, what the RBI is for, and what 1991 and GST refer to. Leave the latest growth rate for a current-affairs pass.

If a question quotes a number from the latest Budget, this page cannot be the source. The Budget changes every year.

What SSC lists

  • The economic scene of India, as named in the syllabus.
  • National income words, the RBI, planning and GST.
  • Poverty, employment and the 1991 reforms at a basic level.
  • Deeper finance and economics, for AAO only, is on the Paper-III pages.

Rules and formulas

Income words

GDP is the market value of final goods and services produced inside the country in a period.
GNP is GDP plus net factor income from abroad.
These are definitions. A current figure for either one has to come from a fresh government release.

Institutions

The Reserve Bank of India was established in 1935 and nationalised in 1949.
The Planning Commission was set up in 1950. NITI Aayog was set up in 2015 and replaced it as the policy think tank.
GST began on 1 July 2017. The constitutional amendment associated with it is the 101st.

1991 and deficits

The 1991 reforms are summarised as liberalisation, privatisation and globalisation.
The FRBM Act, 2003, is the fiscal-discipline law. Its targets have been amended since, so do not memorise an old deficit number as if it were still the statute.
A revenue deficit is revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts. A fiscal deficit is the borrowing requirement of the government. A primary deficit is the fiscal deficit minus interest payments.

Work

Disguised unemployment means more people are engaged than the work requires, so some have near-zero marginal product. It is the type associated with agriculture in these questions.
The Green Revolution in India is associated, in exam answers, with high-yielding wheat and with M.S. Swaminathan among Indian scientists.

Common traps

GDP is not “all money in the country”. It is production of final goods and services.

NITI Aayog did not exist in 1950. The Planning Commission did.

GST’s start date is 1 July 2017, not 2016.

Do not quote a deficit target from an old guidebook. Check the latest FRBM amendment or the Budget speech if a number is asked.

Worked examples

Read the solution only after you have tried the question. Options are in exam style.

Example 1
GST in India was rolled out on

  1. 1 April 2016
  2. 1 July 2017
  3. 15 August 2017
  4. 26 January 2018

Solution

  1. The date used for the rollout is 1 July 2017.
  2. The constitutional change is the 101st Amendment.

Answer: B. 1 April is the start of many financial years, which is why it appears as a trap.

Example 2
The Reserve Bank of India was established in

  1. 1935
  2. 1947
  3. 1949
  4. 1950

Solution

  1. 1935 is the establishment.
  2. 1949 is the nationalisation.
  3. 1950 is the Planning Commission.

Answer: A. If the question says nationalised, the year changes to 1949.

Example 3
NITI Aayog was established in

  1. 1950
  2. 1991
  3. 2015
  4. 2017

Solution

  1. 2015 is the year NITI Aayog was set up.
  2. 1950 is the Planning Commission. 1991 is the reform year. 2017 is GST.

Answer: C. NITI Aayog replaced the Planning Commission as the government’s think tank.

Example 4
Disguised unemployment is best described as

  1. people refusing every job
  2. more workers than the work needs, with some adding almost no output
  3. unemployment only in large factories
  4. a person between two jobs for a week

Solution

  1. The extra workers look employed.
  2. Their marginal contribution is close to zero.
  3. A short gap between jobs is frictional unemployment, a different type.

Answer: B. Frictional, structural, cyclical and disguised are the four types worth separating.
